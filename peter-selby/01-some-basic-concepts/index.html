From Arithmetic to Algebra

Algebra is a logical outgrowth of arithmetic. Most algebraic problems are built on the four fundamental operations you already know:

The Four Fundamental Operations
OperationSymbolResult is called
Addition\(+\)Sum
Subtraction\(-\)Difference
Multiplication\(\times\)Product
Division\(\div\)Quotient

The key difference? In algebra, letters are used to represent numbers whose values we don't yet know. Finding these unknown values is one of the central activities in algebra.

Key Insight

Algebra lets us translate long word statements into brief mathematical expressions. For example, "the sum of five times a number and two times the same number is equal to seven times the number" becomes simply:

$$5n + 2n = 7n$$

Axioms of Equality

An axiom is a basic assumption accepted as true without proof. The following axioms of equality are fundamental to all algebraic manipulation:

Axioms of Equality

1. Addition: If equals are added to equals, the sums are equal.

$$\text{If } a = b, \text{ then } a + c = b + c$$

2. Subtraction: If equals are subtracted from equals, the differences are equal.

$$\text{If } a = b, \text{ then } a - c = b - c$$

3. Multiplication: If equals are multiplied by equals, the products are equal.

$$\text{If } a = b, \text{ then } a \cdot c = b \cdot c$$

4. Division: If equals are divided by equals, the quotients are equal (provided the divisor is not zero).

$$\text{If } a = b \text{ and } c \neq 0, \text{ then } \frac{a}{c} = \frac{b}{c}$$

5. Transitivity: If \(a = c\) and \(b = c\), then \(a = b\).

Caution

Division by zero is undefined. Expressions like \(\dfrac{5}{0}\) and \(\dfrac{x}{0}\) are meaningless. When a letter appears in the denominator, you must ensure it does not represent zero. For instance, in \(\dfrac{6}{x - 3}\), the value \(x = 3\) would make the denominator zero, resulting in an impossible division.

Algebraic Representation

Algebra allows us to convert word statements into precise mathematical shorthand. Here is a quick reference for translating everyday phrases:

Example

"Three times a number subtracted from eight times the same number equals five times the number."

Let \(n\) represent the number:

$$8n - 3n = 5n$$
Example

"The sum of \(x\) and \(ab\), divided by the quantity three minus \(y\)."

Multiplication Notation

In arithmetic, multiplication is shown with the \(\times\) sign. In algebra, we avoid \(\times\) because it could be confused with the letter \(x\). Instead, we use these equivalent notations:

Ways to Write "8 times n"
$$8 \times n \;=\; 8 \cdot n \;=\; 8(n) \;=\; 8n$$

The last form, \(8n\), with the multiplication sign omitted entirely, is preferred in algebra.

For two numbers, parentheses are often preferred over a raised dot to avoid confusion with a decimal point. Thus, "5 times 7 times \(y\)" may be written as \((5)(7)y\) or simply \(35y\).

Similarly, the division symbol \(\div\) is rarely used in algebra. Instead, we write fractions:

$$x \div y \;=\; \frac{x}{y} \;=\; x:y$$

Terms, Factors, and Coefficients

Definition — Term

A term is either a single number or letter, or the product (or quotient) of several numbers or letters. Examples: \(7\), \(\;5a\), \(\;xyz\), \(\;\dfrac{3ay}{2}\).

Definition — Factor

A factor is one of the individual numbers or letters multiplied together to form a term. In \(5c^2d\), the factors are \(5\), \(c^2\), and \(d\).

  • Numerical factor: a digit (e.g., the 7 in \(7xyz\))
  • Literal factor: a letter (e.g., the \(x\), \(y\), \(z\) in \(7xyz\))
Definition — Coefficient

Any factor (or group of factors) is called the coefficient of the remaining factors. In \(4ab\):

  • \(4\) is the numerical coefficient of \(ab\)
  • \(4a\) is the coefficient of \(b\)
  • \(ab\) is the literal coefficient of \(4\)

If a letter has no written coefficient, its numerical coefficient is understood to be 1. Thus \(a\) means \(1 \cdot a\).

The Commutative Law

The commutative law states that the result of addition or multiplication is the same regardless of the order of the numbers.

Commutative Law

For Addition:

$$a + b = b + a$$

For Multiplication:

$$a \cdot b = b \cdot a$$
Examples

\(4 + 9 = 9 + 4 = 13\)

\(x + y = y + x\)

\(3 \cdot 7 = 7 \cdot 3 = 21\)

\(ab = ba\)

Caution — Does NOT Apply to Subtraction or Division

The commutative law does not hold for subtraction or division:

$$7 - 3 \neq 3 - 7 \qquad \qquad 8 \div 4 \neq 4 \div 8$$

In general: \(b - a \neq a - b\) and \(\dfrac{a}{b} \neq \dfrac{b}{a}\).

The Associative Law

The associative law states that the sum (or product) of three or more quantities is the same regardless of how we group them.

Associative Law

For Addition:

$$(a + b) + c = a + (b + c) = a + b + c$$

For Multiplication:

$$(a \cdot b) \cdot c = a \cdot (b \cdot c) = a \cdot b \cdot c$$
Examples

\((2 + 5) + 8 = 2 + (5 + 8) = 15\)

\(2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 = (2 \cdot 3) \cdot 4 = 2 \cdot (3 \cdot 4) = 24\)

\(c \cdot d \cdot f = c(df) = (cd)f = cdf\)

Caution — Does NOT Apply to Subtraction or Division

The associative law does not hold for subtraction or division:

$$(10 - 5) - 2 = 3 \quad \text{but} \quad 10 - (5 - 2) = 7$$ $$(24 \div 6) \div 2 = 2 \quad \text{but} \quad 24 \div (6 \div 2) = 8$$

The Distributive Law

The distributive law connects multiplication with addition (or subtraction). It states that multiplying a single factor by a sum is the same as multiplying the factor by each term and then adding the results.

The Distributive Law
$$a(b + c) = ab + ac$$

Extended for more terms:

$$a(b + c + d) = ab + ac + ad$$
Examples

Two terms:

$$2(3 + 4) = 2 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot 4 = 6 + 8 = 14$$ $$a(a + b) = a^2 + ab$$

With coefficients:

$$2b(ab + bc) = 2ab^2 + 2b^2c$$

Extended (three terms):

$$2a(2a + 3b - 4ad) = 4a^2 + 6ab - 8a^2d$$ $$3ab(a^2 - 2ad + b) = 3a^3b - 6a^2bd + 3ab^2$$
Key Insight

Notice the exponent rule: when a factor is multiplied by itself, the exponents add. For instance, \(a \cdot a = a^2\) and \(b \cdot b^2 = b^3\). We will explore exponent rules more deeply in Chapter 6.

Order of Operations

Since we cannot perform every operation at once, we follow a strict order:

Rules for Order of Operations
  1. Parentheses first: Evaluate expressions inside parentheses (innermost first).
  2. Exponents next: Evaluate powers.
  3. Multiplication and Division: Work left to right.
  4. Addition and Subtraction: Work left to right.
Step-by-Step Example

Evaluate: \(6 + 3(2) - \dfrac{4}{2}\)

Step 1 — Multiply: \(3(2) = 6\)

Step 2 — Divide: \(\dfrac{4}{2} = 2\)

Step 3 — Add and subtract left to right: \(6 + 6 - 2 = 10\)

Parentheses

Parentheses serve three important roles in algebra:

Three Uses of Parentheses
  1. Expressing multiplication without a times sign: \(2(3)\), \((2)3\), or \((2)(3)\).
  2. Grouping terms to be treated as a single number: \(2(x + y)\) means "twice the sum of \(x\) and \(y\)."
  3. Controlling the order of operations: \(4(3 + 2) = 4 \cdot 5 = 20\), but without parentheses, \(4 \cdot 3 + 2 = 12 + 2 = 14\).
Caution

Parentheses can completely change the meaning of an expression!

$$4(3 + 2) = 20 \qquad \text{vs.} \qquad 4 \cdot 3 + 2 = 14$$

Always evaluate the inside of parentheses first.

Exponents

When a factor is multiplied by itself repeatedly, we use exponent notation to express it concisely.

Exponent Notation
$$\underbrace{a \cdot a \cdot a \cdots a}_{n \text{ factors}} = a^n$$

Here, \(a\) is the base, \(n\) is the exponent, and the result \(a^n\) is the power.

Examples
  • \(a \cdot a = a^2\) — read "a squared" or "a to the second power"
  • \(2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 2^3 = 8\) — read "2 cubed" or "2 to the third power"
  • \(y \cdot y \cdot y \cdot y \cdot y = y^5\)
  • \((a + b)(a + b) = (a+b)^2\)
Key Insight

An exponent applies only to the factor directly before it. In \(bx^3\), the exponent 3 applies to \(x\) alone, giving \(b \cdot x \cdot x \cdot x\). If you want the exponent to apply to both \(b\) and \(x\), you must write \((bx)^3\).

Key Insight

If no exponent is written, it is understood to be 1. Thus \(y = y^1\) and \(4 = 4^1\).

Combining Like Terms

Like terms are terms that have the same literal parts (same letters with the same exponents). Only like terms can be combined.

Definition — Like Terms

Terms are like if they have identical literal coefficients (the same variables raised to the same powers). To combine them, add or subtract their numerical coefficients.

Examples

Can be combined:

$$3a + 4a = 7a \qquad \qquad 6x^2 - 2x^2 = 4x^2$$

Cannot be combined:

$$3a + 5b \quad \text{(unlike terms — different letters)}$$ $$2x + 3x^2 \quad \text{(unlike terms — different exponents)}$$
Simplification Example

Simplify: \(6a + 2a^2 - 3a - a^2\)

Group like terms:

$$= (6a - 3a) + (2a^2 - a^2) = 3a + a^2$$
Simplification Example

Simplify: \(4a + 3b + 6a - b + 7\)

Combine like terms: \((4a + 6a) + (3b - b) + 7\)

$$= 10a + 2b + 7$$

Worked Problems

Problem 1 — Evaluate an Expression

Evaluate \(3(a + b) + 2a - \dfrac{b}{2}\) when \(a = 5\) and \(b = 4\).

Solution

Step 1 — Substitute:

$$3(5 + 4) + 2(5) - \frac{4}{2}$$

Step 2 — Parentheses:

$$3(9) + 2(5) - \frac{4}{2}$$

Step 3 — Multiply and divide:

$$27 + 10 - 2$$

Step 4 — Add and subtract:

$$= \boxed{35}$$
Problem 2 — Simplify by Combining Like Terms

Simplify: \(6a + 2a^2 - 3a - a^2\)

Solution

Group the like terms:

$$= (6a - 3a) + (2a^2 - a^2)$$

Combine the numerical coefficients:

$$= \boxed{3a + a^2}$$
Problem 3 — Apply the Distributive Law

Expand: \(4ab(2ab + 3ac - cd)\)

Solution

Distribute \(4ab\) to each term inside the parentheses:

$$4ab \cdot 2ab + 4ab \cdot 3ac - 4ab \cdot cd$$

Multiply the numerical coefficients and combine the literal factors:

$$= 8a^2b^2 + 12a^2bc - 4abcd$$ $$= \boxed{8a^2b^2 + 12a^2bc - 4abcd}$$
Problem 4 — Translate Words to Algebra

Express algebraically: "twice the sum of \(b\) and 18, decreased by 9."

Solution

"The sum of \(b\) and 18" → \((b + 18)\)

"Twice the sum" → \(2(b + 18)\)

"Decreased by 9" → subtract 9

$$= \boxed{2(b + 18) - 9}$$
Problem 5 — Identify Base, Exponent, and Power

In the equation \(x^2 = y\), identify the base, the exponent, and the power.

Solution
  • The base is \(x\) — the repeated factor.
  • The exponent is \(2\) — it tells us \(x\) is used as a factor twice.
  • The power is \(y\) — the result of the multiplication \(x \cdot x\).
Problem 6 — Full Simplification

Simplify: \(2(a + b) - a + 3b\)

Solution

Step 1 — Distribute:

$$2a + 2b - a + 3b$$

Step 2 — Combine like terms:

$$= (2a - a) + (2b + 3b)$$ $$= \boxed{a + 5b}$$
