1. Algebraic Fractions

In algebra, a fraction is simply another way of writing a division. The general form is

$$\frac{a}{b}, \quad b \neq 0$$

where \(a\) is the numerator and \(b\) is the denominator. The fraction bar acts both as a division sign and as a grouping symbol: every term in the numerator is divided by the denominator.

🔑 Definition — Rational Expression

A rational expression (algebraic fraction) is any expression that can be written as \(\dfrac{P}{Q}\), where \(P\) and \(Q\) are polynomials and \(Q \neq 0\).

A fraction is undefined whenever its denominator equals zero.

📋 Rules for Zero in Fractions
Rule Statement Example
Rule 1 \(\dfrac{0}{b} = 0\) provided \(b \neq 0\) \(\dfrac{0}{7} = 0\)
Rule 2 \(\dfrac{0}{0}\) is indeterminate No unique value exists
Rule 3 \(\dfrac{a}{0}\) is undefined (meaningless) \(\dfrac{5}{0}\) is undefined
✅ Example

For what value of \(x\) is \(\dfrac{x - 4}{2 + x}\) equal to zero? For what value is it undefined?

  • The fraction equals zero when the numerator is zero: \(x - 4 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 4\).
  • The fraction is undefined when the denominator is zero: \(2 + x = 0 \Rightarrow x = -2\).

2. Reducing Fractions

A fraction is in lowest terms when the numerator and denominator share no common factor other than 1. To reduce, we factor both parts and cancel what they share.

🔑 Fundamental Principle of Fractions

If \(k \neq 0\), then

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{a \cdot k}{b \cdot k} = \frac{a / k}{b / k}$$

Multiplying or dividing both numerator and denominator by the same non-zero quantity does not change the fraction's value.

Reducing Monomials

Divide both numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor (GCF).

✅ Example — Monomial Reduction
$$\frac{6x^2 y}{3xy} = \frac{6x^2 y \;\div\; 3xy}{3xy \;\div\; 3xy} = \frac{2x}{1} = 2x$$

Reducing with Polynomial Factors

Factor the numerator and denominator completely, then cancel common binomial (or polynomial) factors.

✅ Example — Polynomial Reduction
$$\frac{3(x + y)}{(x + y)(x - y)} = \frac{3\,\cancel{(x + y)}}{\cancel{(x + y)}\,(x - y)} = \frac{3}{x - y}$$

Visualizing the Simplification Process

⚠️ Caution — Common Mistakes When Reducing
  1. Do not subtract the same number from both numerator and denominator. \(\frac{3}{4} \neq \frac{3-1}{4-1} = \frac{2}{3}\).
  2. Do not add the same number to both numerator and denominator. \(\frac{1}{3} \neq \frac{1+2}{3+2} = \frac{3}{5}\).
  3. Do not cancel individual terms of a polynomial — only factors. For example, \(\dfrac{x^2 - 6x}{5x - 30}\) cannot be simplified by cancelling the \(x\) in the first term alone. Instead, factor first: $$\frac{x^2 - 6x}{5x - 30} = \frac{x(x - 6)}{5(x - 6)} = \frac{x}{5}$$

3. Signs of Fractions

Every fraction carries three signs: the sign of the numerator, the sign of the denominator, and the sign of the fraction itself. When any sign is omitted, it is understood to be positive.

📋 Rule — Changing Signs

Any two of the three signs of a fraction may be changed without changing its value:

$$\frac{-a}{b} = \frac{a}{-b} = -\frac{a}{b}$$

All three forms are equal. Changing only one sign changes the value.

✅ Example — Verifying Sign Changes

All four fractions below have the value \(3\):

$$+\frac{+6}{+2} = +\frac{-6}{-2} = -\frac{+6}{-2} = -\frac{-6}{+2} = 3$$

Signs with Binomial Denominators

To change the order of terms in a binomial denominator, multiply the denominator by \(-1\) and compensate by changing one other sign.

✅ Example

Rewrite \(\dfrac{a - 3}{c - d}\) with denominator \(d - c\):

$$\frac{a - 3}{c - d} = \frac{-(a - 3)}{-(c - d)} = \frac{3 - a}{d - c}$$

We multiplied both numerator and denominator by \(-1\), which changes two signs and preserves the value.

4. Multiplying Fractions

The product of two fractions equals the product of the numerators over the product of the denominators.

📋 Multiplication Rule
$$\frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ac}{bd}$$

Best practice: Factor and cancel common factors before multiplying. This keeps numbers small and avoids reducing at the end.

✅ Example — Cancel First, Then Multiply
$$\frac{3ab^2}{4} \cdot \frac{12}{18a^2} = \frac{3ab^2 \cdot 12}{4 \cdot 18a^2}$$

Factor the numbers: \(12 = 3 \cdot 4\) and \(18 = 3 \cdot 6\). Cancel:

$$= \frac{\cancel{3}\,a\,b^2 \cdot \cancel{4} \cdot \cancel{3}}{\cancel{4} \cdot \cancel{3} \cdot 6 \cdot a \cdot \cancel{a}} = \frac{b^2}{6a} \cdot \frac{1}{1} = \frac{b^2}{2a}$$

Multiplying with Polynomials

Factor every numerator and denominator completely before cancelling.

✅ Example
$$\frac{x + y}{8} \cdot \frac{10}{x^2 - y^2} = \frac{(x + y) \cdot 10}{8 \cdot (x + y)(x - y)} = \frac{10}{8(x - y)} = \frac{5}{4(x - y)}$$

5. Dividing Fractions

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal (the fraction flipped).

📋 Division Rule (Invert and Multiply)
$$\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{d}{c} = \frac{ad}{bc}$$
💡 Key Insight — Reciprocals
  • The reciprocal of \(\dfrac{a}{b}\) is \(\dfrac{b}{a}\).
  • The product of a number and its reciprocal is always \(1\): \(\;\dfrac{a}{b} \cdot \dfrac{b}{a} = 1\).
  • The reciprocal of a whole number \(n\) is \(\dfrac{1}{n}\).
✅ Example
$$\frac{4a^2}{3ab} \div \frac{7a}{6b^2} = \frac{4a^2}{3ab} \cdot \frac{6b^2}{7a} = \frac{4 \cdot 6 \cdot a^2 \cdot b^2}{3 \cdot 7 \cdot a \cdot b \cdot a} = \frac{24a^2 b^2}{21a^2 b} = \frac{8b}{7}$$

6. Finding the LCD (Least Common Denominator)

Before adding or subtracting fractions with different denominators, we need a common denominator. The most efficient choice is the lowest common denominator (LCD) — the smallest expression that every denominator divides into evenly.

📋 Rule — Finding the LCD
1 Write each denominator in prime factored form, using exponents for repeated factors.
2 List every distinct prime factor that appears.
3 Raise each factor to its highest exponent found in any denominator. Multiply the results.
✅ Example — Numerical LCD

Find the LCD for fractions with denominators 12, 15, 9, and 21.

$$12 = 2^2 \cdot 3, \quad 15 = 3 \cdot 5, \quad 9 = 3^2, \quad 21 = 3 \cdot 7$$

Distinct primes: \(2, 3, 5, 7\). Use highest powers: \(2^2,\; 3^2,\; 5^1,\; 7^1\).

$$\text{LCD} = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 = 4 \cdot 9 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 = 1260$$
✅ Example — Algebraic LCD

Find the LCD for \(\dfrac{1}{3(a-2)}\), \(\;\dfrac{1}{6a}\), and \(\;\dfrac{1}{2(a+2)}\).

Factored denominators: \(3(a-2)\), \(\;2 \cdot 3 \cdot a\), and \(\;2(a+2)\).

$$\text{LCD} = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot a \cdot (a - 2)(a + 2) = 6a(a-2)(a+2)$$

7. Adding and Subtracting Fractions

📋 Addition / Subtraction Rules

Same denominator:

$$\frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{b} = \frac{a + c}{b}$$

Different denominators:

$$\frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ad + bc}{bd}$$

After combining, always reduce the result to lowest terms.

1 Change each fraction to an equivalent fraction with the LCD.
2 Add (or subtract) the numerators; place over the common denominator.
3 Reduce the resulting fraction to lowest terms.
✅ Example

Combine \(\;\dfrac{x}{2y^2} + \dfrac{4}{y}\). The LCD is \(2y^2\).

$$\frac{x}{2y^2} + \frac{4}{y} = \frac{x}{2y^2} + \frac{4 \cdot 2y}{2y^2} = \frac{x + 8y}{2y^2} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{xy - 8}{2y^2}$$
✅ Example — Polynomial Denominators

Combine \(\;\dfrac{a-3}{a^2 - 25} + \dfrac{6}{a+5} - \dfrac{3}{a-5}\).

Factor: \(a^2 - 25 = (a-5)(a+5)\). LCD \(= (a-5)(a+5)\).

$$= \frac{(a-3) + 6(a-5) - 3(a+5)}{(a-5)(a+5)} = \frac{a - 3 + 6a - 30 - 3a - 15}{(a-5)(a+5)}$$ $$= \frac{4a - 48}{(a-5)(a+5)} = \frac{4(a - 12)}{(a-5)(a+5)}$$

8. Complex Fractions

A complex fraction has one or more fractions in its numerator, denominator, or both.

🔑 Two Methods for Simplifying

Method 1 (LCD method): Multiply both the overall numerator and overall denominator by the LCD of all the "little" fractions.

Method 2 (Combine-then-divide): Simplify the numerator into a single fraction, simplify the denominator into a single fraction, then divide numerator by denominator.

✅ Example — Method 1 (LCD Method)

Simplify \(\;\dfrac{\;\dfrac{x^2 - y^2}{4}\;}{\;\dfrac{x + y}{2}\;}\).

The LCD of the inner fractions is \(4\). Multiply top and bottom by \(4\):

$$\frac{\frac{x^2 - y^2}{4} \cdot 4}{\frac{x+y}{2} \cdot 4} = \frac{x^2 - y^2}{2(x+y)} = \frac{(x+y)(x-y)}{2(x+y)} = \frac{x - y}{2}$$
✅ Example — Method 2 (Combine Then Divide)

Simplify \(\;\dfrac{\;\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{3}\;}{\;\dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{4}\;}\).

Combine top: \(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{5}{6}\). Combine bottom: \(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{4}\).

$$\frac{\;\frac{5}{6}\;}{\;\frac{1}{4}\;} = \frac{5}{6} \cdot \frac{4}{1} = \frac{20}{6} = \frac{10}{3}$$

9. Mixed Numbers and Expressions

A mixed number (or mixed expression) combines a whole-number part with a fractional part, such as \(3\frac{2}{5}\) or \(a + \frac{k}{y}\).

📋 Converting Mixed to Improper Fraction

Add the whole-number part and the fraction using a common denominator:

$$a + \frac{k}{y} = \frac{ay}{y} + \frac{k}{y} = \frac{ay + k}{y}$$
📋 Converting Improper Fraction to Mixed Expression

Divide the numerator by the denominator:

$$\frac{4x^2 + 8x - 3}{2x} = \frac{4x^2}{2x} + \frac{8x}{2x} - \frac{3}{2x} = 2x + 4 - \frac{3}{2x}$$
✅ Example — Polynomial Long Division

Convert \(\;\dfrac{x^2 + 3x + 4}{x + 1}\) to a mixed expression.

Divide \(x + 1\) into \(x^2 + 3x + 4\):

$$\frac{x^2 + 3x + 4}{x + 1} = x + 2 + \frac{2}{x + 1}$$

The remainder \(2\) goes over the divisor \((x+1)\).

Key Insights

💡 Insight 1 — Factor First, Always

The single most important step in simplifying algebraic fractions is factoring. Before reducing, multiplying, dividing, or finding the LCD, factor every numerator and denominator completely. Factoring reveals the common factors that can be cancelled.

💡 Insight 2 — Only Factors Cancel, Never Terms

You can cancel \((x+2)\) from \(\dfrac{(x+2)(x-3)}{(x+2)}\) because \((x+2)\) is a factor of both. But you cannot cancel the \(x\) from \(\dfrac{x+2}{x+5}\) because \(x\) is a term, not a factor, of the numerator and denominator.

💡 Insight 3 — Changing Two Signs Preserves Value

When you need \((a - b)\) but have \((b - a)\), note that \(b - a = -(a - b)\). Introduce the factor of \(-1\) and compensate by changing one other sign. This trick is essential when finding the LCD for fractions with "reversed" binomial denominators.

💡 Insight 4 — Division Is Multiplication in Disguise

Every fraction division can be rewritten as multiplication by the reciprocal. This reduces every division problem to a multiplication problem — factor, cancel, multiply. Master multiplication and you've mastered division too.

Worked Problems

📝 Problem 1 — Reduce

Reduce to lowest terms: \(\;\dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x + 2}\)

✅ Solution

Factor the numerator using the difference of squares:

$$\frac{x^2 - 4}{x + 2} = \frac{(x + 2)(x - 2)}{x + 2}$$

Cancel the common factor \((x + 2)\):

$$= \frac{\cancel{(x + 2)}\,(x - 2)}{\cancel{(x + 2)}} = \boxed{x - 2} \quad (x \neq -2)$$
📝 Problem 2 — Multiply

Multiply: \(\;\dfrac{3a}{4b} \cdot \dfrac{8b^2}{9a^2}\)

✅ Solution

Multiply numerators and denominators, then cancel common factors:

$$\frac{3a}{4b} \cdot \frac{8b^2}{9a^2} = \frac{3a \cdot 8b^2}{4b \cdot 9a^2} = \frac{24ab^2}{36a^2 b}$$

Divide numerator and denominator by \(12ab\):

$$= \frac{24ab^2 \div 12ab}{36a^2 b \div 12ab} = \boxed{\frac{2b}{3a}}$$
📝 Problem 3 — Add

Add: \(\;\dfrac{2}{x} + \dfrac{3}{y}\)

✅ Solution

The LCD is \(xy\). Convert each fraction:

$$\frac{2}{x} + \frac{3}{y} = \frac{2 \cdot y}{x \cdot y} + \frac{3 \cdot x}{y \cdot x} = \frac{2y}{xy} + \frac{3x}{xy}$$

Combine over the common denominator:

$$= \boxed{\frac{2y + 3x}{xy}}$$
📝 Problem 4 — Subtract

Subtract: \(\;\dfrac{5}{x - 1} - \dfrac{3}{x + 1}\)

✅ Solution

The LCD is \((x - 1)(x + 1)\). Convert each fraction:

$$\frac{5}{x - 1} - \frac{3}{x + 1} = \frac{5(x + 1)}{(x - 1)(x + 1)} - \frac{3(x - 1)}{(x - 1)(x + 1)}$$

Expand the numerators and combine:

$$= \frac{5x + 5 - 3x + 3}{(x - 1)(x + 1)} = \boxed{\frac{2x + 8}{(x - 1)(x + 1)}}$$
📝 Problem 5 — Simplify a Complex Fraction

Simplify: \(\;\dfrac{\;\dfrac{1}{a} + \dfrac{1}{b}\;}{\;\dfrac{1}{a} - \dfrac{1}{b}\;}\)

✅ Solution

Method 1 (LCD): The LCD of all inner fractions is \(ab\). Multiply top and bottom by \(ab\):

$$\frac{\left(\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b}\right) \cdot ab}{\left(\frac{1}{a} - \frac{1}{b}\right) \cdot ab} = \frac{\frac{ab}{a} + \frac{ab}{b}}{\frac{ab}{a} - \frac{ab}{b}} = \frac{b + a}{b - a}$$ $$= \boxed{\frac{a + b}{b - a}}$$

Note: this can also be written as \(-\dfrac{a+b}{a-b}\).

📝 Problem 6 — Divide

Divide: \(\;\dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x + 1} \div \dfrac{x - 3}{x^2 + x}\)

✅ Solution

Invert the divisor and multiply:

$$\frac{x^2 - 9}{x + 1} \div \frac{x - 3}{x^2 + x} = \frac{x^2 - 9}{x + 1} \cdot \frac{x^2 + x}{x - 3}$$

Factor everything:

$$= \frac{(x + 3)(x - 3)}{(x + 1)} \cdot \frac{x(x + 1)}{(x - 3)}$$

Cancel common factors \((x - 3)\) and \((x + 1)\):

$$= \frac{(x + 3)\cancel{(x - 3)}}{\cancel{(x + 1)}} \cdot \frac{x\cancel{(x + 1)}}{\cancel{(x - 3)}} = \boxed{x(x + 3)}$$

📋 Finding the LCD — Quick Steps
  1. Factor each denominator into primes (numerical and algebraic).
  2. List every distinct factor.
  3. Use the highest power of each factor. Multiply them all together.
⚠️ Three Traps to Avoid
  1. Cancelling terms instead of factors. — \(\dfrac{x + 3}{x + 5} \neq \dfrac{3}{5}\)
  2. Forgetting restrictions. — Always note values that make a denominator zero.
  3. Distributing signs incorrectly. — When subtracting fractions, the minus sign applies to the entire numerator: \(\frac{5}{x} - \frac{a+b}{x} = \frac{5 - a - b}{x}\), not \(\frac{5 - a + b}{x}\).
