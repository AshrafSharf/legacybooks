Chapter 11

Ratio, Proportion, and Variation

Comparing quantities and discovering how variables change together

What You'll Learn

  • Write ratios and reduce them to lowest terms
  • Set up and solve proportions using cross multiplication
  • Recognize and work with direct proportion (direct variation)
  • Recognize and work with inverse proportion (inverse variation)
  • Handle joint variation and combined variation
  • Apply ratios and proportions to real-world problems: scale drawings, similar triangles, mixtures, and more

1. Ratio

One of the most natural things humans do with numbers is compare them. A ratio gives us a way to compare two quantities by division rather than by subtraction.

Definition — Ratio

If \(a\) and \(b\) are quantities expressed in the same unit of measure, the ratio of \(a\) to \(b\) is the quotient

$$\frac{a}{b} \qquad (b \neq 0)$$

A ratio may also be written as \(a : b\) or \(a \div b\). Always express a ratio in lowest terms.

For example, if one person is 22 years old and another is 33 years old, the ratio of their ages is

$$\frac{22}{33} = \frac{2}{3} \qquad \text{or} \quad 2:3$$
Key Insight

Because a ratio is a quotient, the units cancel. The ratio \(20\text{ ft} : 50\text{ ft}\) is simply \(2:5\). However, both quantities must be in the same unit before you form the ratio. For instance, to compare 3 yards with 7 feet, first convert: \(3 \text{ yd} = 9 \text{ ft}\), giving a ratio of \(9:7\).

Quick Examples

ComparisonRatio (lowest terms)
15 days to 35 days\(3:7\)

2. Proportion

Definition — Proportion

A proportion is an equation stating that two ratios are equal:

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \qquad \text{or equivalently} \quad a : b = c : d$$

We read this as “\(a\) is to \(b\) as \(c\) is to \(d\).”

Means and Extremes

In the proportion \(a : b = c : d\):

  • Extremes — the outer terms: \(a\) and \(d\)
  • Means — the inner terms: \(b\) and \(c\)
Cross-Multiplication Theorem

If \(b \neq 0\) and \(d \neq 0\), and if \(\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}\), then

$$a \cdot d = b \cdot c$$

The product of the extremes equals the product of the means. This is the fundamental tool for solving proportions.

Proof sketch: Start with \(\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}\). Multiply both sides by \(bd\):

$$\frac{a}{b} \cdot bd = \frac{c}{d} \cdot bd \implies a \cdot d = b \cdot c \quad \checkmark$$
Key Insight

A proportion is just an equation, so all the rules of equations apply. Cross multiplication is simply a shortcut for clearing the denominators. You can also interchange means or extremes: if \(a:b = c:d\), then \(a:c = b:d\), \(d:b = c:a\), and \(d:c = b:a\).

3. Solving Proportions

When three of the four terms in a proportion are known, cross multiplication lets us find the missing term.

Example — Solving a Basic Proportion

Problem: Solve \(\dfrac{3}{x} = \dfrac{9}{15}\).

1
Cross multiply: \(3 \cdot 15 = 9 \cdot x\)
2
Simplify: \(45 = 9x\)
3
Divide both sides by 9: \(x = 5\)

Check: \(\frac{3}{5} = \frac{9}{15} = \frac{3}{5}\) ✓

Example — Proportion with an Expression

Problem: Solve \(\dfrac{5}{x - 2} = \dfrac{4}{7}\).

1
Cross multiply: \(5 \cdot 7 = 4(x - 2)\)
2
Simplify: \(35 = 4x - 8\)
3
Add 8: \(43 = 4x\)
4
Divide: \(x = \dfrac{43}{4} = 10\tfrac{3}{4}\)

4. Direct Variation (Direct Proportion)

When two quantities increase (or decrease) together at a constant rate, they are in direct variation.

Definition — Direct Variation

If two variables \(x\) and \(y\) are related so that

$$\frac{y}{x} = k \qquad \text{(constant)}$$

then \(y\) varies directly as \(x\). Equivalently,

$$y = kx$$

The constant \(k\) is called the constant of variation (or constant of proportionality).

Consider traveling at a constant speed of 60 mph. The relationship between time \(t\) and distance \(d\) is:

\(t\) (hours)12361012
\(d\) (miles)60120180360600720

Every ratio \(\frac{t}{d}\) equals \(\frac{1}{60}\). The distance varies directly as time with \(k = 60\), giving \(d = 60t\).

Figure 1. The graph of direct variation is always a straight line through the origin.

Key Insight — Recognizing Direct Variation

If doubling one quantity doubles the other, you have direct variation. The graph is a straight line through the origin, and the slope of that line is the constant of variation \(k\).

5. Inverse Variation (Inverse Proportion)

Sometimes when one quantity increases, the other decreases so that their product stays constant. This is inverse variation.

Definition — Inverse Variation

If two variables \(x\) and \(y\) are related so that

$$x \cdot y = k \qquad \text{(constant)}$$

then \(y\) varies inversely as \(x\). Equivalently,

$$y = \frac{k}{x}$$

The constant \(k\) is again called the constant of variation.

Consider the relationship between the pressure \(p\) and volume \(V\) of a gas at constant temperature:

\(p\) (psi)5040201510
\(V\) (cu. in.)2002505006671000
\(p \cdot V\)10,00010,00010,00010,00010,000

Every product \(p \cdot V = 10{,}000\). Pressure and volume are inversely proportional with \(k = 10{,}000\).

Figure 2. The graph of inverse variation is a hyperbola — as \(x\) grows, \(y\) shrinks toward zero.

Common Mistake

Don’t confuse inverse variation with negative variation. In inverse variation, as one variable increases the other decreases, but both can remain positive. The key test: check whether the product \(xy\) is constant (inverse) or the quotient \(y/x\) is constant (direct).

6. Joint and Combined Variation

Joint Variation

Definition — Joint Variation

If \(x\) varies directly as the product of two or more variables, \(x\) is said to vary jointly as those variables:

$$x = k \cdot a \cdot b \qquad \text{or equivalently} \quad \frac{x}{ab} = k$$

Notice that \(x\) varies directly with each variable when the others are held constant.

For example, the area of a triangle \(A = \frac{1}{2}bh\) shows that area varies jointly as the base \(b\) and height \(h\) (with \(k = \frac{1}{2}\)).

Combined Variation

Definition — Combined Variation

Combined variation mixes direct and inverse variation in a single relationship:

$$x = \frac{k \cdot y}{z}$$

Here \(x\) varies directly as \(y\) and inversely as \(z\).

Summary of Variation Types
TypeEquationMeaning
Direct\(y = kx\)\(y/x\) is constant
Inverse\(y = k/x\)\(xy\) is constant
Joint\(z = kxy\)\(z/(xy)\) is constant
Combined\(z = kx/y\)\(zy/x\) is constant

7. Application: Similar Triangles

Similar triangles have the same shape but not necessarily the same size. Their corresponding angles are equal, and their corresponding sides are proportional.

Figure 3. Similar triangles: sides of the smaller triangle are exactly half the corresponding sides of the larger triangle (ratio 1:2).

If triangle \(ABC\) has sides 6, 8, 10 and is similar to triangle \(DEF\) with sides 3, 4, 5, then the ratio of corresponding sides is constant:

$$\frac{3}{6} = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2}$$
Key Insight — Applications of Proportion

Proportions appear everywhere: scale drawings (architectural plans, maps), similar figures (geometry), unit conversion, recipe scaling, and gear ratios. In each case, two ratios are set equal and the unknown is found by cross multiplication.

Worked Problems

Problem 1 — Solving a Proportion

If \(\dfrac{3}{x} = \dfrac{9}{15}\), find \(x\).

Solution
1
Cross multiply: \(3 \cdot 15 = 9 \cdot x\)
2
Simplify: \(45 = 9x\)
3
Divide both sides by 9: \(\;x = \dfrac{45}{9} = 5\)

Check: \(\frac{3}{5} = 0.6\) and \(\frac{9}{15} = 0.6\) ✓

Problem 2 — Direct Variation

\(y\) varies directly as \(x\). If \(y = 12\) when \(x = 4\), find \(y\) when \(x = 7\).

Solution
1
Write the equation: \(y = kx\)
2
Find \(k\): \(12 = k \cdot 4 \implies k = 3\)
3
Write the specific equation: \(y = 3x\)
4
Substitute \(x = 7\): \(y = 3(7) = 21\)

Answer: \(y = 21\) when \(x = 7\).

Problem 3 — Inverse Variation

\(y\) varies inversely as \(x\). If \(y = 6\) when \(x = 8\), find \(y\) when \(x = 12\).

Solution
1
Write the equation: \(xy = k\)
2
Find \(k\): \(8 \cdot 6 = 48\), so \(k = 48\)
3
Write the specific equation: \(xy = 48\), or \(y = \dfrac{48}{x}\)
4
Substitute \(x = 12\): \(y = \dfrac{48}{12} = 4\)

Answer: \(y = 4\) when \(x = 12\).

Problem 4 — Joint Variation

\(z\) varies jointly as \(x\) and \(y\). If \(z = 60\) when \(x = 3\) and \(y = 4\), find \(z\) when \(x = 5\) and \(y = 2\).

Solution
1
Write the equation: \(z = kxy\)
2
Find \(k\): \(60 = k(3)(4) = 12k \implies k = 5\)
3
Write the specific equation: \(z = 5xy\)
4
Substitute \(x = 5,\; y = 2\): \(z = 5(5)(2) = 50\)

Answer: \(z = 50\) when \(x = 5\) and \(y = 2\).

Problem 5 — Ratio / Mixture Problem

Paint is mixed in the ratio \(3:5\) (Part A to Part B). If you need 24 liters of paint in total, how much of each part do you need?

Solution
1
Total parts: \(3 + 5 = 8\) parts
2
Value of one part: \(\dfrac{24}{8} = 3\) liters per part
3
Part A: \(3 \times 3 = 9\) liters
4
Part B: \(5 \times 3 = 15\) liters

Check: \(9 + 15 = 24\) liters, and \(9:15 = 3:5\) ✓

Problem 6 — Similar Triangles

Two similar triangles have corresponding sides. The first triangle has sides 3, 4, 5. The second has sides 6, \(?\), 10. Find the missing side.

Solution
1
Identify the scale factor. Compare known corresponding sides: \(\dfrac{6}{3} = 2\) and \(\dfrac{10}{5} = 2\).
2
The scale factor is 2. The missing side corresponds to 4 in the first triangle.
3
Set up the proportion: \(\dfrac{4}{?} = \dfrac{3}{6}\)
4
Cross multiply: \(4 \cdot 6 = 3 \cdot {?} \implies 24 = 3 \cdot {?} \implies {?} = 8\)

Answer: The missing side is 8. The second triangle has sides 6, 8, 10.

Quick Reference

Ratio

$$\frac{a}{b} = a : b$$

Always in lowest terms; same units required.

Proportion

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \implies ad = bc$$

Cross-multiply to solve for unknowns.

Direct Variation

$$y = kx \quad \left(\frac{y}{x} = k\right)$$

Quotient is constant; line through origin.

Inverse Variation

$$y = \frac{k}{x} \quad (xy = k)$$

Product is constant; hyperbola shape.

Joint Variation

$$z = kxy$$

Direct variation with a product of variables.

Combined Variation

$$z = \frac{kx}{y}$$

Mix of direct and inverse.

The Four-Step Method for Variation Problems
  1. Set up the equation with the constant \(k\) (decide the type of variation).
  2. Find \(k\) using the given pair(s) of values.
  3. Write the specific equation with the known value of \(k\).
  4. Substitute the new values and solve for the unknown.
