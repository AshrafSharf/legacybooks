1.6 Introduction to Square Numbers

Textbook pages 25–27 · 5 figures

More often we write like this:

\(4 = 16\)

This says "4 squared is 16"

The 2 at the top stands for squared and it indicates the number of times the number 4

appears in the product \((4 = 4 \times 4 = 16)\). The numbers 1, 4, 9, 16, ... are all square numbers (also called perfect square numbers). Each of them is made up of the product of same two factors. A natural number n is called a square number, if we can find another natural number m

such that \(n = m\) .

1.6.1 Some more special properties of square numbers

  1. The square of a natural number other than 1, is either a multiple of 3 or exceeds a ­multiple of 3 by 1.
  2. The square of a natural number, other than 1, is either a multiple of 4 or exceeds a ­multiple of 4 by 1.
  3. The remainder of a perfect square when divided by 3, is either 0 or 1 but never 2.
  4. The remainder of a perfect square, when divided by 4, is either 0 or 1 but never 2 and 3.
  5. When a perfect square number is divided by 8, the remainder is either 0 or 1 or 4, but

will never be equal to 2, 3, 5, 6 or 7.
