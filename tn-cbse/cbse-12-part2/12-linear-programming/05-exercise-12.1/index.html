Ten linear programming problems to solve graphically with the Corner Point Method

Exercise 12.1

Exercise 12.1

Solve the following Linear Programming Problems graphically:

1

Maximise \(Z = 3x + 4y\)
subject to the constraints: \(x + y \le 4,\ x \ge 0,\ y \ge 0\).

Maximum \(Z = 16\) at \((0, 4)\)

2

Minimise \(Z = -3x + 4y\)
subject to \(x + 2y \le 8,\ 3x + 2y \le 12,\ x \ge 0,\ y \ge 0\).

Minimum \(Z = -12\) at \((4, 0)\)

3

Maximise \(Z = 5x + 3y\)
subject to \(3x + 5y \le 15,\ 5x + 2y \le 10,\ x \ge 0,\ y \ge 0\).

Maximum \(Z = \dfrac{235}{19}\) at \(\left(\dfrac{20}{19}, \dfrac{45}{19}\right)\)

4

Minimise \(Z = 3x + 5y\)
such that \(x + 3y \ge 3,\ x + y \ge 2,\ x, y \ge 0\).

Minimum \(Z = 7\) at \(\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)\)

5

Maximise \(Z = 3x + 2y\)
subject to \(x + 2y \le 10,\ 3x + y \le 15,\ x, y \ge 0\).

Maximum \(Z = 18\) at \((4, 3)\)

6

Minimise \(Z = x + 2y\)
subject to \(2x + y \ge 3,\ x + 2y \ge 6,\ x, y \ge 0\).

Show that the minimum of Z occurs at more than two points.

Minimum \(Z = 6\) at all the points on the line segment joining the points \((6, 0)\) and \((0, 3)\).

7

Minimise and Maximise \(Z = 5x + 10y\)
subject to \(x + 2y \le 120,\ x + y \ge 60,\ x - 2y \ge 0,\ x, y \ge 0\).

Minimum \(Z = 300\) at \((60, 0)\);

Maximum \(Z = 600\) at all the points on the line segment joining the points \((120, 0)\) and \((60, 30)\).

8

Minimise and Maximise \(Z = x + 2y\)
subject to \(x + 2y \ge 100,\ 2x - y \le 0,\ 2x + y \le 200;\ x, y \ge 0\).

Minimum \(Z = 100\) at all the points on the line segment joining the points \((0, 50)\) and \((20, 40)\);

Maximum \(Z = 400\) at \((0, 200)\)

9

Maximise \(Z = -x + 2y\), subject to the constraints:
\(x \ge 3,\ x + y \ge 5,\ x + 2y \ge 6,\ y \ge 0\).

Z has no maximum value

10

Maximise \(Z = x + y\), subject to \(x - y \le -1,\ -x + y \le 0,\ x, y \ge 0\).

No feasible region, hence no maximum value of Z.
