11.1 Introduction

Bringing the power of vectors to the geometry of space — lines, planes and the distances between them

From Two Dimensions to Space

In Class XI, while studying Analytical Geometry in two dimensions, and the introduction to three dimensional geometry, we confined to the Cartesian methods only. In the previous chapter of this book, we have studied some basic concepts of vectors. We will now use vector algebra to three dimensional geometry. The purpose of this approach to 3-dimensional geometry is that it makes the study simple and elegant*.

In this chapter, we shall study the direction cosines and direction ratios of a line joining two points and also discuss about the equations of lines and planes in space under different conditions, angle between two lines, two planes, a line and a plane, shortest distance between two skew lines and distance of a point from a plane.

Most of the above results are obtained in vector form. Nevertheless, we shall also translate these results in the Cartesian form which, at times, presents a more clear geometric and analytic picture of the situation.

Key Insight

A vector equation describes a geometric object without reference to any particular set of axes; the Cartesian form then gives the same object in coordinates. Throughout this chapter you will see each result in both forms.

* For various activities in three dimensional geometry, one may refer to the Book “A Hand Book for designing Mathematics Laboratory in Schools”, NCERT, 2005.
