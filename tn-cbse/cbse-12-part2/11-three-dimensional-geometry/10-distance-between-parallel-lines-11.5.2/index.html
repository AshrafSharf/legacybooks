11.5.2 Distance between parallel lines

When two lines share a direction, a cross product with \(\vec{b}\) measures the gap between them

In This Section

  • Parallel lines are coplanar; the distance between them is the perpendicular TP
  • Deriving \(d = \left|\dfrac{\vec{b} \times (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1)}{|\vec{b}|}\right|\) using the cross product
  • Example 9: shortest distance between two skew lines
  • Example 10: distance between two parallel lines

Deriving the Formula

If two lines \(l_1\) and \(l_2\) are parallel, then they are coplanar. Let the lines be given by

\[ \vec{r} = \vec{a}_1 + \lambda\vec{b} \tag{1} \]

and

\[ \vec{r} = \vec{a}_2 + \mu\vec{b} \tag{2} \]

where, \(\vec{a}_1\) is the position vector of a point S on \(l_1\) and \(\vec{a}_2\) is the position vector of a point T on \(l_2\) (Fig 11.7).

As \(l_1, l_2\) are coplanar, if the foot of the perpendicular from T on the line \(l_1\) is P, then the distance between the lines \(l_1\) and \(l_2 = |\mathrm{TP}|\).

Let \(\theta\) be the angle between the vectors \(\overrightarrow{\mathrm{ST}}\) and \(\vec{b}\). Then

\[ \vec{b} \times \overrightarrow{\mathrm{ST}} = \left(|\vec{b}|\,|\overrightarrow{\mathrm{ST}}|\sin\theta\right)\hat{n} \tag{3} \]

where \(\hat{n}\) is the unit vector perpendicular to the plane of the lines \(l_1\) and \(l_2\).

But \(\overrightarrow{\mathrm{ST}} = \vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1\). Therefore, from (3), we get

\[ \vec{b} \times (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1) = |\vec{b}|\,\mathrm{PT}\,\hat{n} \qquad (\text{since } \mathrm{PT} = \mathrm{ST}\sin\theta) \]

i.e.,

\[ |\vec{b} \times (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1)| = |\vec{b}|\,\mathrm{PT}\cdot 1 \qquad (\text{as } |\hat{n}| = 1) \]

Hence, the distance between the given parallel lines is

Distance between parallel lines
\[ d = |\overrightarrow{\mathrm{PT}}| = \left| \frac{\vec{b} \times (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1)}{|\vec{b}|} \right| \]

Worked Examples

Example 9

Find the shortest distance between the lines \(l_1\) and \(l_2\) whose vector equations are

\[ \vec{r} = \hat{i} + \hat{j} + \lambda\left(2\hat{i} - \hat{j} + \hat{k}\right) \tag{1} \]

and

\[ \vec{r} = 2\hat{i} + \hat{j} - \hat{k} + \mu\left(3\hat{i} - 5\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}\right) \tag{2} \]

Solution Comparing (1) and (2) with \(\vec{r} = \vec{a}_1 + \lambda\vec{b}_1\) and \(\vec{r} = \vec{a}_2 + \mu\vec{b}_2\) respectively, we get

\[ \vec{a}_1 = \hat{i} + \hat{j},\quad \vec{b}_1 = 2\hat{i} - \hat{j} + \hat{k} \] \[ \vec{a}_2 = 2\hat{i} + \hat{j} - \hat{k} \quad\text{and}\quad \vec{b}_2 = 3\hat{i} - 5\hat{j} + 2\hat{k} \]

Therefore

\[ \vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1 = \hat{i} - \hat{k} \]

and

\[ \vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2 = \left(2\hat{i} - \hat{j} + \hat{k}\right) \times \left(3\hat{i} - 5\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}\right) = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 2 & -1 & 1 \\ 3 & -5 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = 3\hat{i} - \hat{j} - 7\hat{k} \]

So

\[ |\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2| = \sqrt{9 + 1 + 49} = \sqrt{59} \]

Hence, the shortest distance between the given lines is given by

\[ d = \left| \frac{(\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2)\cdot(\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1)}{|\vec{b}_1 \times \vec{b}_2|} \right| = \frac{|3 - 0 + 7|}{\sqrt{59}} = \frac{10}{\sqrt{59}} \]
Example 10

Find the distance between the lines \(l_1\) and \(l_2\) given by

\[ \vec{r} = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 4\hat{k} + \lambda\left(2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}\right) \]

and

\[ \vec{r} = 3\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} - 5\hat{k} + \mu\left(2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}\right) \]

Solution The two lines are parallel (Why?) We have

\[ \vec{a}_1 = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 4\hat{k},\quad \vec{a}_2 = 3\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} - 5\hat{k} \quad\text{and}\quad \vec{b} = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 6\hat{k} \]

Therefore, the distance between the lines is given by

\[ d = \left| \frac{\vec{b} \times (\vec{a}_2 - \vec{a}_1)}{|\vec{b}|} \right| = \left| \frac{\begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 2 & 3 & 6 \\ 2 & 1 & -1 \end{vmatrix}}{\sqrt{4 + 9 + 36}} \right| \]

or

\[ d = \frac{\left|-9\hat{i} + 14\hat{j} - 4\hat{k}\right|}{\sqrt{49}} = \frac{\sqrt{293}}{\sqrt{49}} = \frac{\sqrt{293}}{7} \]
Why are the lines in Example 10 parallel?

Both lines have the same direction vector \(2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}\), so their direction ratios are proportional (in fact equal).
