Chapter 11 · Three Dimensional Geometry

Exercise 11.1

Practise finding direction cosines from angles, ratios and points — and test for collinearity

Exercise 11.1

Tools you need

\(l = \cos\alpha,\ m = \cos\beta,\ n = \cos\gamma\) with \(l^2 + m^2 + n^2 = 1\); from direction ratios \(a, b, c\): \(l = \dfrac{a}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}}\) etc.; through two points the direction ratios are \(x_2 - x_1,\ y_2 - y_1,\ z_2 - z_1\).

1

If a line makes angles 90°, 135°, 45° with the \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\)-axes respectively, find its direction cosines.

\( 0,\ \dfrac{-1}{\sqrt{2}},\ \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \)

2

Find the direction cosines of a line which makes equal angles with the coordinate axes.

\( \pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}},\ \pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}},\ \pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \)

3

If a line has the direction ratios \(-18, 12, -4\), then what are its direction cosines?

\( \dfrac{-9}{11},\ \dfrac{6}{11},\ \dfrac{-2}{11} \)

4

Show that the points \((2, 3, 4)\), \((-1, -2, 1)\), \((5, 8, 7)\) are collinear.

5

Find the direction cosines of the sides of the triangle whose vertices are \((3, 5, -4)\), \((-1, 1, 2)\) and \((-5, -5, -2)\).

\( \dfrac{-2}{\sqrt{17}},\ \dfrac{-2}{\sqrt{17}},\ \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{17}};\quad \dfrac{-2}{\sqrt{17}},\ \dfrac{-3}{\sqrt{17}},\ \dfrac{-2}{\sqrt{17}};\quad \dfrac{4}{\sqrt{42}},\ \dfrac{5}{\sqrt{42}},\ \dfrac{-1}{\sqrt{42}} \)
