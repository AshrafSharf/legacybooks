11.5 Shortest Distance between Two Lines

Intersecting, parallel or skew — how close can two lines in space get?

In This Section

  • Shortest distance for intersecting and for parallel lines
  • Skew lines: lines that are neither intersecting nor parallel (non coplanar)
  • A room as a model: the ceiling diagonal and the wall diagonal are skew
  • What “shortest distance” means, and why it is perpendicular to both skew lines

Three Ways Two Lines Can Sit in Space

If two lines in space intersect at a point, then the shortest distance between them is zero. Also, if two lines in space are parallel, then the shortest distance between them will be the perpendicular distance, i.e. the length of the perpendicular drawn from a point on one line onto the other line.

Further, in a space, there are lines which are neither intersecting nor parallel. In fact, such pair of lines are non coplanar and are called skew lines.

Definition — Skew lines

Lines in space which are neither intersecting nor parallel are called skew lines. Such a pair of lines is non coplanar.

For example, let us consider a room of size 1, 3, 2 units along \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\)-axes respectively (Fig 11.5).

The line GE that goes diagonally across the ceiling and the line DB passes through one corner of the ceiling directly above A and goes diagonally down the wall. These lines are skew because they are not parallel and also never meet.

What “Shortest Distance” Means

Definition — Shortest distance between two lines

By the shortest distance between two lines we mean the join of a point in one line with one point on the other line so that the length of the segment so obtained is the smallest.

Key Fact

For skew lines, the line of the shortest distance will be perpendicular to both the lines.
