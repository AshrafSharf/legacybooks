10.1 Introduction

How tall are you, and where should the ball go? Two kinds of answers: scalars and vectors

  • Quantities with only a magnitude — scalars
  • Quantities with a magnitude and a direction — vectors

  • What this chapter will study: operations on vectors and their algebraic and geometric properties

Scalars and Vectors

In our day to day life, we come across many queries such as – What is your height? How should a football player hit the ball to give a pass to another player of his team?

Observe that a possible answer to the first query may be 1.6 meters, a quantity that involves only one value (magnitude) which is a real number. Such quantities are called scalars.

However, an answer to the second query is a quantity (called force) which involves muscular strength (magnitude) and direction (in which another player is positioned). Such quantities are called vectors.

Scalars and Vectors in a Nutshell

In mathematics, physics and engineering, we frequently come across with both types of quantities, namely, scalar quantities such as length, mass, time, distance, speed, area, volume, temperature, work, money, voltage, density, resistance etc. and vector quantities like displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, weight, momentum, electric field intensity etc.

What Lies Ahead

In this chapter, we will study some of the basic concepts about vectors, various operations on vectors, and their algebraic and geometric properties. These two type of properties, when considered together give a full realisation to the concept of vectors, and lead to their vital applicability in various areas as mentioned above.

Key Insight

A scalar answers “how much?”; a vector answers “how much, and which way?”. The football pass needs both how hard to kick and where to aim.
