13.5.1 Partition of a sample space

Cutting the sample space into non-overlapping pieces that together cover everything

In This Section

  • The three conditions for events \(E_1, E_2, \ldots, E_n\) to form a partition of S
  • Pairwise disjoint, exhaustive and with nonzero probabilities
  • Simple examples: an event and its complement; the four regions of a Venn diagram

What is a Partition?

Definition — Partition of a Sample Space

A set of events \(E_1, E_2, \ldots, E_n\) is said to represent a partition of the sample space S if

  1. \(E_i \cap E_j = \phi,\; i \neq j,\; i, j = 1, 2, 3, \ldots, n\)
  2. \(E_1 \cup E_2 \cup \ldots \cup E_n = S\) and
  3. \(P(E_i) \gt 0\) for all \(i = 1, 2, \ldots, n\).

In other words, the events \(E_1, E_2, \ldots, E_n\) represent a partition of the sample space S if they are pairwise disjoint, exhaustive and have nonzero probabilities.

Examples of Partitions

As an example, we see that any nonempty event E and its complement E′ form a partition of the sample space S since they satisfy \(E \cap E' = \phi\) and \(E \cup E' = S\).

From the Venn diagram in Fig 13.3, one can easily observe that if E and F are any two events associated with a sample space S, then the set \(\{E \cap F',\, E \cap F,\, E' \cap F,\, E' \cap F'\}\) is a partition of the sample space S.

Note

It may be mentioned that the partition of a sample space is not unique. There can be several partitions of the same sample space.
