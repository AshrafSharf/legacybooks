7.8.2 First fundamental theorem of integral calculus

Differentiate the area function and you get back the function you started with

In This Section

  • The statement of the first fundamental theorem of integral calculus
  • Why it shows that differentiation and integration are inverse processes

The First Fundamental Theorem

Theorem 1

Let \(f\) be a continuous function on the closed interval \([a, b]\) and let \( \mathrm{A}(x) \) be the area function. Then \( \mathrm{A}'(x) = f(x) \), for all \( x \in [a, b] \).

Recall that the area function is \( \mathrm{A}(x) = \displaystyle\int_a^x f(x)\,dx \). So the theorem says

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left( \int_a^x f(t)\,dt \right) = f(x). \]
Key Insight

The area function \( \mathrm{A} \) is itself an anti derivative of \(f\). The rate at which the area grows as the right-hand ordinate moves is exactly the height \( f(x) \) of the curve at that point.
