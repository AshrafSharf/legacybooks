7.2.1 Some properties of indefinite integral

The rules that let integrals split, scale and combine — and the art of integrating by inspection

In This Section

  • Differentiation and integration as inverse operations

Properties of Indefinite Integrals

In this sub section, we shall derive some properties of indefinite integrals.

Property (I) — Differentiation and integration are inverses

The process of differentiation and integration are inverses of each other in the sense of the following results:

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\int f(x)\,dx = f(x) \] \[ \text{and}\qquad \int f'(x)\,dx = f(x) + \text{C},\ \text{where C is any arbitrary constant.} \]

Proof Let F be any anti derivative of \( f \), i.e.,

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\text{F}(x) = f(x) \]

Then \( \int f(x)\,dx = \text{F}(x) + \text{C} \). Therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dx}\int f(x)\,dx &= \frac{d}{dx}\big(\text{F}(x) + \text{C}\big) \\ &= \frac{d}{dx}\text{F}(x) = f(x) \end{aligned} \]

Similarly, we note that

\[ f'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}f(x) \] \[ \text{and hence}\qquad \int f'(x)\,dx = f(x) + \text{C} \]

where C is arbitrary constant called constant of integration.

Equivalent Integrals

Property (II) — Equivalence

Two indefinite integrals with the same derivative lead to the same family of curves and so they are equivalent.

Proof Let \( f \) and \( g \) be two functions such that

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\int f(x)\,dx = \frac{d}{dx}\int g(x)\,dx \] \[ \text{or}\qquad \frac{d}{dx}\left[\int f(x)\,dx - \int g(x)\,dx\right] = 0 \] \[ \text{Hence}\qquad \int f(x)\,dx - \int g(x)\,dx = \text{C},\ \text{where C is any real number} \quad (\text{Why?}) \] \[ \text{or}\qquad \int f(x)\,dx = \int g(x)\,dx + \text{C} \]

So the families of curves \( \left\{\int f(x)\,dx + \text{C}_1,\ \text{C}_1 \in \mathbf{R}\right\} \) and \( \left\{\int g(x)\,dx + \text{C}_2,\ \text{C}_2 \in \mathbf{R}\right\} \) are identical.

Hence, in this sense, \( \int f(x)\,dx \) and \( \int g(x)\,dx \) are equivalent.

Note

The equivalence of the families \( \left\{\int f(x)\,dx + \text{C}_1,\ \text{C}_1 \in \mathbf{R}\right\} \) and \( \left\{\int g(x)\,dx + \text{C}_2,\ \text{C}_2 \in \mathbf{R}\right\} \) is customarily expressed by writing \( \int f(x)\,dx = \int g(x)\,dx \), without mentioning the parameter.

Linearity of the Integral

Property (III) — Integral of a sum
\[ \int \big[f(x) + g(x)\big]\,dx = \int f(x)\,dx + \int g(x)\,dx \]

Proof By Property (I), we have

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left[\int [f(x) + g(x)]\,dx\right] = f(x) + g(x) \tag{1} \]

On the otherhand, we find that

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dx}\left[\int f(x)\,dx + \int g(x)\,dx\right] &= \frac{d}{dx}\int f(x)\,dx + \frac{d}{dx}\int g(x)\,dx \\ &= f(x) + g(x) \end{aligned} \tag{2} \]

Thus, in view of Property (II), it follows by (1) and (2) that

\[ \int \big(f(x) + g(x)\big)\,dx = \int f(x)\,dx + \int g(x)\,dx. \]
Property (IV) — Constant multiple

For any real number \( k \),

\[ \int k\,f(x)\,dx = k\int f(x)\,dx \]

Proof By the Property (I), \( \dfrac{d}{dx}\displaystyle\int k\,f(x)\,dx = k\,f(x) \).

\[ \text{Also}\qquad \frac{d}{dx}\left[k\int f(x)\,dx\right] = k\frac{d}{dx}\int f(x)\,dx = k\,f(x) \]

Therefore, using the Property (II), we have \( \int k\,f(x)\,dx = k\int f(x)\,dx \).

Property (V) — Generalisation

Properties (III) and (IV) can be generalised to a finite number of functions \( f_1, f_2, \ldots, f_n \) and the real numbers, \( k_1, k_2, \ldots, k_n \) giving

\[ \begin{aligned} &\int \big[k_1 f_1(x) + k_2 f_2(x) + \ldots + k_n f_n(x)\big]\,dx \\ &\quad= k_1\int f_1(x)\,dx + k_2\int f_2(x)\,dx + \ldots + k_n\int f_n(x)\,dx. \end{aligned} \]

Integration by Inspection

To find an anti derivative of a given function, we search intuitively for a function whose derivative is the given function. The search for the requisite function for finding an anti derivative is known as integration by the method of inspection. We illustrate it through some examples.

Example 1

Write an anti derivative for each of the following functions using the method of inspection:

(i) \( \cos 2x \)     (ii) \( 3x^2 + 4x^3 \)     (iii) \( \dfrac{1}{x},\ x \ne 0 \)

Solution

(i) We look for a function whose derivative is \( \cos 2x \). Recall that

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\sin 2x = 2\cos 2x \] \[ \text{or}\qquad \cos 2x = \frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dx}(\sin 2x) = \frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sin 2x\right) \]

Therefore, an anti derivative of \( \cos 2x \) is \( \dfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x \).

(ii) We look for a function whose derivative is \( 3x^2 + 4x^3 \). Note that

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left(x^3 + x^4\right) = 3x^2 + 4x^3. \]

Therefore, an anti derivative of \( 3x^2 + 4x^3 \) is \( x^3 + x^4 \).

(iii) We know that

\[ \frac{d}{dx}(\log x) = \frac{1}{x},\ x \gt 0 \quad\text{and}\quad \frac{d}{dx}[\log(-x)] = \frac{1}{-x}(-1) = \frac{1}{x},\ x \lt 0 \]

Combining above, we get \( \dfrac{d}{dx}\big(\log|x|\big) = \dfrac{1}{x},\ x \ne 0 \).

Therefore, \( \displaystyle\int \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \log|x| \) is one of the anti derivatives of \( \dfrac{1}{x} \).

Example 2

Find the following integrals:

(i) \( \displaystyle\int \frac{x^3 - 1}{x^2}\,dx \)     (ii) \( \displaystyle\int \left(x^{\frac{2}{3}} + 1\right)dx \)     (iii) \( \displaystyle\int \left(x^{\frac{3}{2}} + 2e^x - \frac{1}{x}\right)dx \)

Solution

(i) We have

\[ \begin{aligned} \int \frac{x^3 - 1}{x^2}\,dx &= \int x\,dx - \int x^{-2}\,dx \qquad (\text{by Property V}) \\ &= \left(\frac{x^{1+1}}{1+1} + \text{C}_1\right) - \left(\frac{x^{-2+1}}{-2+1} + \text{C}_2\right);\quad \text{C}_1, \text{C}_2 \text{ are constants of integration} \\ &= \frac{x^2}{2} + \text{C}_1 - \frac{x^{-1}}{-1} - \text{C}_2 = \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{1}{x} + \text{C}_1 - \text{C}_2 \\ &= \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{1}{x} + \text{C},\ \text{where C} = \text{C}_1 - \text{C}_2 \text{ is another constant of integration.} \end{aligned} \]
Note

From now onwards, we shall write only one constant of integration in the final answer.

(ii) We have

\[ \begin{aligned} \int \left(x^{\frac{2}{3}} + 1\right)dx &= \int x^{\frac{2}{3}}\,dx + \int dx \\ &= \frac{x^{\frac{2}{3}+1}}{\frac{2}{3}+1} + x + \text{C} = \frac{3}{5}x^{\frac{5}{3}} + x + \text{C} \end{aligned} \]

(iii) We have

\[ \begin{aligned} \int \left(x^{\frac{3}{2}} + 2e^x - \frac{1}{x}\right)dx &= \int x^{\frac{3}{2}}\,dx + \int 2e^x\,dx - \int \frac{1}{x}\,dx \\ &= \frac{x^{\frac{3}{2}+1}}{\frac{3}{2}+1} + 2e^x - \log|x| + \text{C} \\ &= \frac{2}{5}x^{\frac{5}{2}} + 2e^x - \log|x| + \text{C} \end{aligned} \]
Example 3

Find the following integrals:

(i) \( \int (\sin x + \cos x)\,dx \)     (ii) \( \int \operatorname{cosec} x\,(\operatorname{cosec} x + \cot x)\,dx \)     (iii) \( \displaystyle\int \frac{1 - \sin x}{\cos^2 x}\,dx \)

Solution

(i) We have

\[ \begin{aligned} \int (\sin x + \cos x)\,dx &= \int \sin x\,dx + \int \cos x\,dx \\ &= -\cos x + \sin x + \text{C} \end{aligned} \]

(ii) We have

\[ \begin{aligned} \int \operatorname{cosec} x\,(\operatorname{cosec} x + \cot x)\,dx &= \int \operatorname{cosec}^2 x\,dx + \int \operatorname{cosec} x \cot x\,dx \\ &= -\cot x - \operatorname{cosec} x + \text{C} \end{aligned} \]

(iii) We have

\[ \begin{aligned} \int \frac{1 - \sin x}{\cos^2 x}\,dx &= \int \frac{1}{\cos^2 x}\,dx - \int \frac{\sin x}{\cos^2 x}\,dx \\ &= \int \sec^2 x\,dx - \int \tan x \sec x\,dx \\ &= \tan x - \sec x + \text{C} \end{aligned} \]
Example 4

Find the anti derivative F of \( f \) defined by \( f(x) = 4x^3 - 6 \), where \( \text{F}(0) = 3 \).

Solution One anti derivative of \( f(x) \) is \( x^4 - 6x \) since

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\left(x^4 - 6x\right) = 4x^3 - 6 \]

Therefore, the anti derivative F is given by

\[ \text{F}(x) = x^4 - 6x + \text{C},\ \text{where C is constant.} \]

Given that \( \text{F}(0) = 3 \), which gives,

\[ 3 = 0 - 6 \times 0 + \text{C} \quad\text{or}\quad \text{C} = 3 \]

Hence, the required anti derivative is the unique function F defined by

\[ \text{F}(x) = x^4 - 6x + 3. \]

Remarks

Remarks

(i) We see that if F is an anti derivative of \( f \), then so is F + C, where C is any constant. Thus, if we know one anti derivative F of a function \( f \), we can write down an infinite number of anti derivatives of \( f \) by adding any constant to F expressed by \( \text{F}(x) + \text{C},\ \text{C} \in \mathbf{R} \). In applications, it is often necessary to satisfy an additional condition which then determines a specific value of C giving unique anti derivative of the given function.

(ii) Sometimes, F is not expressible in terms of elementary functions viz., polynomial, logarithmic, exponential, trigonometric functions and their inverses etc. We are therefore blocked for finding \( \int f(x)\,dx \). For example, it is not possible to find \( \int e^{-x^2}\,dx \) by inspection since we can not find a function whose derivative is \( e^{-x^2} \).

(iii) When the variable of integration is denoted by a variable other than \( x \), the integral formulae are modified accordingly. For instance

\[ \int y^4\,dy = \frac{y^{4+1}}{4+1} + \text{C} = \frac{1}{5}y^5 + \text{C} \]
