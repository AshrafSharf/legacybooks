7.7 Definite Integral

From a family of antiderivatives to a single number — meet the definite integral

In This Section

  • What a definite integral is and how it is written
  • The lower limit and upper limit of integration
  • Two ways to introduce it: as the limit of a sum, or through an anti derivative

The Definite Integral

In the previous sections, we have studied about the indefinite integrals and discussed few methods of finding them including integrals of some special functions. In this section, we shall study what is called definite integral of a function.

Definition — Definite Integral

The definite integral has a unique value. A definite integral is denoted by

\[ \int_a^b f(x)\,dx \]

where \(a\) is called the lower limit of the integral and \(b\) is called the upper limit of the integral.

The definite integral is introduced either as the limit of a sum or, if it has an anti derivative F in the interval \([a, b]\), then its value is the difference between the values of F at the end points, i.e.,

\[ \mathrm{F}(b) - \mathrm{F}(a). \]
Key Insight

An indefinite integral \( \int f(x)\,dx \) is a whole family of functions (it carries the constant C); a definite integral \( \int_a^b f(x)\,dx \) is a single number.
