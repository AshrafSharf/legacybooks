Imagine a line of length 16 units. Erasing half of it would result in

\(4 4 2 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 2\)

\((2 \div 2) \div 2 = 2 \div 2 = 2 \times 2 = 2 \times 2 = 2 = 4\) units.

Halving 16 cm three times may be written as,

\(2 \div 2 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 2 = 2 = 2\) units.

\(4 3 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 1\)

From this we can see that

\(2 \div 2 = 2 = 2\) .

\(4 3 4 - 3 1\)

What is \(2 \div 2\) in powers of 2? In a generalised form,

\(n \div n = n\) ,

\(a b a - b\)

where \(n \ne 0\) and a and b are counting numbers and \(a > b\).

what is 2 ?

Let us define 2 in a way that the generalised form above holds true.

\(0 4 - 4 4 4 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2\)

\(2 = 2 = 2 \div 2 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 1\).

In fact for any letter number a

\(2 = 2 = 2 \div 2 = 1\).

\(0 a - a a a\)

In general,

\(x \div x = x = x\) , and so

\(a a a - a 0\)

\(1 = x\) ,

where \(x \ne 0\) and a is a counting number.

When Zero is in Power!

When a line of length 2 units is halved 5 times,

\(4 5 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 2 \div 2 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 = \frac{1}{2}\) units.

Using the generalised form, we get \(2 \div 2 = 2 = 2\) .

\(4 5 (4 - 5) - 1\)

So, \(2 = \frac{1}{2}\) .
