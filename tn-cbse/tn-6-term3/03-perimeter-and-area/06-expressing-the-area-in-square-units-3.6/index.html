Chapter 3 · Perimeter and Area

3.6 Expressing the Area in Square Units

Textbook pages 13–14 · 2 figures

Consider a square of side 1 cm. Therefore, its area is 1 sq. cm (\(1\text{ cm}^{2}\)). Divide one of its sides into 10 equal parts. One such part is equal to 1 mm. We know that 1 cm \(= 10\) mm. That is a square of side 1 cm is made up of 100 small squares with 1 mm square area each. Therefore, the side of this square is 10 mm and the area of this square \(=\) side \(\times\) side \(= 10\text{ mm} \times 10\text{ mm} = 100\) sq. mm (\(100\text{ mm}^{2}\)). Therefore, the area of a square with 1 cm side is \(1\text{ cm}^{2} = 100\text{ mm}^{2}\).

Similarly, the other conversions can also be done. For example,

  1. \(1\text{ cm}^{2} = 10\text{ mm} \times 10\text{ mm}\)

    \(= 100\text{ mm}^{2}\)

  2. \(1\text{ m}^{2} = 100\text{ cm} \times 100\text{ cm}\)

    \(= 10{,}000\text{ cm}^{2}\)

  3. \(1\text{ km}^{2} = 1000\text{ m} \times 1000\text{ m}\)

    \(= 10{,}00{,}000\text{ m}^{2}\)

Example 15 Fill in the blanks.

  1. \(2\text{ cm}^{2} = \underline{\qquad}\text{ mm}^{2}\)
  2. \(18\text{ m}^{2} = \underline{\qquad}\text{ cm}^{2}\)
  3. \(5\text{ km}^{2} = \underline{\qquad}\text{ m}^{2}\)

Solution

  1. \(2\text{ cm}^{2} = 2 \times 100 = 200\text{ mm}^{2}\)
  2. \(18\text{ m}^{2} = 18 \times 10000 = 1{,}80{,}000\text{ cm}^{2}\)
  3. \(5\text{ km}^{2} = 5 \times 1000000 = 50{,}00{,}000\text{ m}^{2}\)
