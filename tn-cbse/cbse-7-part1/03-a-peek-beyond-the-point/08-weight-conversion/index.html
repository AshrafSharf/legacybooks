Weight Conversion

Textbook pages 22–23 · 2 figures

Let us look at kilograms (kg). We know that 1 kg \(= 1000\) gram (g). We can say that

\[1\ \text{g} = \frac{1}{1000}\ \text{kg} = 0.001\ \text{kg}.\]

How many kilograms is 5 g?

\[5\ \text{g} = \frac{5}{1000}\ \text{kg} = 0.005\ \text{kg}.\]

How many kilograms is 10 g?

\[10\ \text{g} = \frac{10}{1000}\ \text{kg} = \frac{1}{100}\ \text{kg} = 0.010\ \text{kg}.\]

As each gram is one-thousandth of a kg, 254 g can be written as

\(254\ \text{g} = \frac{254}{1000}\ \text{kg}\)

\(= \left(\frac{200}{1000} + \frac{50}{1000} + \frac{4}{1000}\right)\ \text{kg}\)

\(= \left(\frac{2}{10} + \frac{5}{100} + \frac{4}{1000}\right)\ \text{kg}\)

\(= 0.254\ \text{kg}.\)
