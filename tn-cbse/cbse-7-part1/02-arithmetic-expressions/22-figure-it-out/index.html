  1. Fill in the blanks with numbers, and boxes with operation signs such that the expressions on both sides are equal.
    1. \(24 + (6 - 4) = 24 + 6 \boxed{\phantom{0}} \underline{\qquad}\)
    2. \(38 + (\underline{\qquad} \boxed{\phantom{0}} \underline{\qquad}) = 38 + 9 - 4\)
    3. \(24 - (6 + 4) = 24 \boxed{\phantom{0}} 6 - 4\)
    4. \(24 - 6 - 4 = 24 - 6 \boxed{\phantom{0}} \underline{\qquad}\)
    5. \(27 - (8 + 3) = 27 \underline{\qquad} 8 \underline{\qquad} 3\)
    6. \(27 - (\underline{\qquad} \boxed{\phantom{0}} \underline{\qquad}) = 27 - 8 + 3\)

    Ans:

    1. \(24 + (6 - 4) = 24 + 6 \boxed{-} \underline{4}\)
    2. \(38 + (\underline{9} \boxed{-} \underline{4}) = 38 + 9 - 4\)
    3. \(24 - (6 + 4) = 24 \boxed{-} 6 - 4\)
    4. \(24 - 6 - 4 = 24 - 6 \boxed{-} \underline{4}\)
    5. \(27 - (8 + 3) = 27 - 8 - 3\)
    6. \(27 - (\underline{8} \boxed{-} \underline{3}) = 27 - 8 + 3\)
  2. Remove the brackets and write the expression having the same value.
    1. \(14 + (12 + 10)\)
    2. \(14 - (12 + 10)\)
    3. \(14 + (12 - 10)\)
    4. \(14 - (12 - 10)\)
    5. \(-14 + 12 - 10\)
    6. \(14 - (-12 - 10)\)

    Ans: (a) and (f)

  3. Find the values of the following expressions. For each pair, first try to guess whether they have the same value. When are the two expressions equal?
    1. \((6 + 10) - 2\) and \(6 + (10 - 2)\)
    2. \(16 - (8 - 3)\) and \((16 - 8) - 3\)
    3. \(27 - (18 + 4)\) and \(27 + (-18 - 4)\)

    Ans:

    1. \((6 + 10) - 2 = 6 + 10 - 2 = 14\)

      \(6 + (10 - 2) = 6 + 10 - 2 = 14\)

      So, \((6 + 10) - 2 = 6 + (10 - 2)\)

    2. \(16 - (8 - 3)\) and \((16 - 8) - 3\)

      \(16 - (8 - 3) = 16 - 8 + 3 = 11\)

      \((16 - 8) - 3 = 16 - 8 - 3 = 5\)

      So, \(16 - (8 - 3) \neq (16 - 8) - 3\)

    3. \(27 - (18 + 4)\) and \(27 + (-18 - 4)\)

      \(27 - (18 + 4) = 27 - 18 - 4 = 5\)

      \(27 + (-18 - 4) = 27 - 18 - 4 = 5\)

      So, \(27 - (18 + 4) = 27 + (-18 - 4)\)

  4. In each of the sets of expressions below, identify those that have the same value. Do not evaluate them, but rather use your understanding of terms.
    1. \(319 + 537\), \(319 - 537\), \(-537 + 319\), \(537 - 319\)
    2. \(87 + 46 - 109\), \(87 + 46 - 109\), \(87 + 46 - 109\), \(87 - 46 + 109\), \(87 - (46 + 109)\), \((87 - 46) + 109\)

    Ans:

    1. \(319 - 537\) and \(-537 + 319\) have the same terms So, \(319 - 537\) and \(-537 + 319\) have the same value.
    2. \(87 + 46 - 109\), \(87 + 46 - 109\), \(87 + 46 - 109\) and also, \(87 - 46 + 109\) and \((87 - 46) + 109\) have the same terms and so have equal values.
  5. Add brackets at appropriate places in the expressions such that they lead to the values indicated.
    1. \(34 - 9 + 12 = 13\)
    2. \(56 - 14 - 8 = 34\)
    3. \(-22 - 12 - 10 + 22 = -22\)

    Ans:

    1. \(34 - (9 + 12) = 34 - 21 = 13\)
    2. \((56 - 14) - 8 = 42 - 8 = 34\)
    3. \(-22 - (12 + 10) + 22 = -22 - 22 + 22 = -22\)
  6. Using only reasoning of how terms change their values, fill the blanks to make the expressions on either side of the equality (=) equal.
    1. \(423 + \underline{\qquad} = 419 + \underline{\qquad}\)
    2. \(207 - 68 = 210 - \underline{\qquad}\)

    Ans:

    1. \(423 + 419 = 419 + 423\) (Commutative Property)
    2. \(207 - 68 = 207 + 3 - 3 - 68 = 207 + 3 - (3 + 68) = 210 - \underline{71}\) (Associative Property)
  7. Using the numbers 2, 3, and 5, and the operators ‘+’ and ‘−’, and brackets, as necessary, generate expressions to give as many different values as possible.

    For example, \(2 - 3 + 5 = 4\) and \(3 - (5 - 2) = 0\)

    Ans: Some of the expressions are

    1. \(3 - 2 - 5 = -4\)
    2. \(5 + 2 + 3 = 10\)
    3. \(5 + 2 - 3 = 4\)
    4. \(2 - (3 + 5) = -6\)

    Form more expressions.

  8. Whenever Jasoda has to subtract 9 from a number, she subtracts 10 and adds 1 to it.

    For example, \(36 - 9 = 26 + 1\)

    1. Do you think she always gets the correct answer? Why?
    2. Can you think of other similar strategies? Give some examples.

    Ans:

    1. Yes

      \(36 - 9 = 36 - (10 - 1) = 36 - 10 + 1 = 26 + 1\)

    2. Yes

      To subtract 8: Subtract 10 and add 2 (since \(-8 = -10 + 2\)).

      Ex: \(55 - 8 = 55 - (10 - 2) = 55 - 10 + 2\)

      More such strategies can be thought of.

  9. Consider the two expressions:
    1. \(73 - 14 + 1\)
    2. \(73 - 14 - 1\)

    For each of these expressions, identify the expressions from the following collection that are equal to it.

    1. \(73 - (14 + 1)\)
    2. \(73 - (14 - 1)\)
    3. \(73 + (-14 + 1)\)
    4. \(73 + (-14 - 1)\)

    Ans: Expressions (b) and (c) are equal to the expression \(73 - 14 + 1\), and expressions (a) and (d) are equal to the expression \(73 - 14 - 1\).

\(5 \times 4 + 3 \neq 5 \times (4 + 3)\). Can you explain why?

Ans:

\(5 \times 4 + 3 = (5 \times 4) + 3 = 23.\)

\(5 \times (4 + 3) = 5 \times 7 = 35\)

So, \(5 \times 4 + 3 \neq 5 \times (4 + 3)\)

Is \(5 \times (4 + 3) = 5 \times (3 + 4) = (3 + 4) \times 5\)?

Ans: Yes, all three expressions are equal.
