Figure it Out (Page 183)

  1. A water tank is filled from a tap. If the tap is open for 1 hour, \(\frac{7}{10}\) of the tank gets filled. How much of the tank is filled if the tap is open for:
    1. \(\frac{1}{3}\) hour — Ans: \(\frac{7}{30}\) part
    2. \(\frac{2}{3}\) hour — Ans: \(\frac{14}{30}\) part
    3. \(\frac{3}{4}\) hour — Ans: \(\frac{21}{40}\) part
    4. \(\frac{7}{10}\) hour — Ans: \(\frac{49}{100}\) part
    5. For the tank to be full, how long should the tap be running? Ans: \(\frac{10}{7}\) hours
  2. The government has taken \(\frac{1}{6}\) of Somu’s land to build a road. What part of the land remains with Somu now? She gives half of the remaining part of the land to her daughter Krishna and \(\frac{1}{3}\) of it to her son Bora. After giving them their shares, she keeps the remaining land for herself.

    Ans: Part of land that remains with Somu \(= \frac{5}{6}\)

    1. What part of the original land did Krishna get? Ans: \(\frac{5}{12}\)
    2. What part of the original land did Bora get? Ans: \(\frac{5}{18}\)
    3. What part of the original land did Somu keep for herself? Ans: \(\frac{5}{36}\)
  3. Find the area of a rectangle of sides \(3\frac{3}{4}\) ft and \(9\frac{3}{5}\) ft. Ans: 36 sq. ft.
  4. Tsewang plants four saplings in a row in his garden. The distance between two saplings is \(\frac{3}{4}\) m. Find the distance between the first and last sapling.

    Ans: \(2\frac{1}{4}\) m

  5. Which is heavier: \(\frac{12}{15}\) of 500 grams or \(\frac{3}{20}\) of 4 kg?

    Ans: \(\frac{3}{20}\) of 4 kg is heavier than \(\frac{12}{15}\) of 500 gm.

Solutions (Page 191)

In a day, the number of times —

  1. the first fountain will fill the cistern is \(1 \div 1 = 1\)
  2. the second fountain will fill the cistern is \(1 \div \frac{1}{2} = 2\) times
  3. the third fountain will fill the cistern is \(1 \div \frac{1}{4} = 4\) times
  4. the fourth fountain will fill the cistern is \(1 \div \frac{1}{5} = 5\) times

The number of times the four fountains together will fill the cistern in a day is

\(= 1 + 2 + 4 + 5 = 12\)
