Exercise 2.9

Multiple Choice Questions

  1. If \(n\) is a natural number then \(\sqrt{n}\) is
    (1) always a natural number. (2) always an irrational number. (3) always a rational number (4) may be rational or irrational
  2. Which of the following is not true?.
    (1) Every rational number is a real number. (2) Every integer is a rational number. (3) Every real number is an irrational number. (4) Every natural number is a whole number.
  3. Which one of the following, regarding sum of two irrational numbers, is true?
    (1) always an irrational number. (2) may be a rational or an irrational number. (3) always a rational number. (4) always an integer.
  4. Which one of the following has a terminating decimal expansion?.
    (1) \(\frac{5}{64}\) (2) \(\frac{8}{9}\) (3) \(\frac{14}{15}\) (4) \(\frac{1}{12}\)
  5. Which one of the following is an irrational number
    (1) \(\sqrt{25}\) (2) \(\sqrt{\frac{9}{4}}\) (3) \(\frac{7}{11}\) (4) \(\pi\)
  6. An irrational number between 2 and 2.5 is
    (1) \(\sqrt{11}\) (2) \(\sqrt{5}\) (3) \(\sqrt{2.5}\) (4) \(\sqrt{8}\)
  7. The smallest rational number by which \(\frac{1}{3}\) should be multiplied so that its decimal expansion terminates with one place of decimal is
    (1) \(\frac{1}{10}\) (2) \(\frac{3}{10}\) (3) 3 (4) 30
  8. If \(\frac{1}{7} = 0.\overline{142857}\) then the value of \(\frac{5}{7}\) is
    (1) \(0.\overline{142857}\) (2) \(0.\overline{714285}\) (3) \(0.\overline{571428}\) (4) \(0.714285\)
  9. Find the odd one out of the following.
    (1) \(\sqrt{32} \times \sqrt{2}\) (2) \(\frac{\sqrt{27}}{\sqrt{3}}\) (3) \(\sqrt{72} \times \sqrt{8}\) (4) \(\frac{\sqrt{54}}{\sqrt{18}}\)
  10. \(0.\overline{34} + 0.3\overline{4} =\)
    (1) \(0.6\overline{87}\) (2) \(0.\overline{68}\) (3) \(0.6\overline{8}\) (4) \(0.68\overline{7}\)
  11. Which of the following statement is false?
    (1) The square root of 25 is 5 or \(-5\) (2) \(-\sqrt{25} = -5\) (3) \(\sqrt{25} = 5\) (4) \(\sqrt{25} = \pm 5\)
  12. Which one of the following is not a rational number?
    (1) \(\sqrt{\frac{8}{18}}\) (2) \(\frac{7}{3}\) (3) \(\sqrt{0.01}\) (4) \(\sqrt{13}\)
  13. \(\sqrt{27} + \sqrt{12} =\)
    (1) \(\sqrt{39}\) (2) \(5\sqrt{6}\) (3) \(5\sqrt{3}\) (4) \(3\sqrt{5}\)
  14. If \(\sqrt{80} = k\sqrt{5}\), then \(k =\)
    (1) 2 (2) 4 (3) 8 (4) 16
  15. \(4\sqrt{7} \times 2\sqrt{3} =\)
    (1) \(6\sqrt{10}\) (2) \(8\sqrt{21}\) (3) \(8\sqrt{10}\) (4) \(6\sqrt{21}\)
  16. When written with a rational denominator, the expression \(\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3\sqrt{2}}\) can be simplified as
    (1) \(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\) (2) \(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\) (3) \(\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\) (4) \(\frac{2}{3}\)
  17. When \(\left(2\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{2}\right)^2\) is simplified, we get
    (1) \(4\sqrt{5} + 2\sqrt{2}\) (2) \(22 - 4\sqrt{10}\) (3) \(8 - 4\sqrt{10}\) (4) \(2\sqrt{10} - 2\)
  18. \((0.000729)^{\frac{-3}{4}} \times (0.09)^{\frac{-3}{4}} = \underline{\qquad}\)
    (1) \(\frac{10^3}{3^3}\) (2) \(\frac{10^5}{3^5}\) (3) \(\frac{10^2}{3^2}\) (4) \(\frac{10^6}{3^6}\)
  19. If \(\sqrt{9^x} = \sqrt[3]{9^2}\), then \(x = \underline{\qquad}\)
    (1) \(\frac{2}{3}\) (2) \(\frac{4}{3}\) (3) \(\frac{1}{3}\) (4) \(\frac{5}{3}\)
  20. The length and breadth of a rectangular plot are \(5 \times 10^5\) and \(4 \times 10^4\) metres respectively. Its area is \(\underline{\qquad}\).
    (1) \(9 \times 10^1\ m^2\) (2) \(9 \times 10^9\ m^2\) (3) \(2 \times 10^{10}\ m^2\) (4) \(20 \times 10^{20}\ m^2\)
