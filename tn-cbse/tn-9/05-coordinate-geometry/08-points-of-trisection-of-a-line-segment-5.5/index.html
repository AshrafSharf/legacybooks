5.5 Points of Trisection of a Line Segment

Textbook pages 24–26 · 6 figures

The mid-point of a line segment is the point of bisection, which means dividing into two parts of equal length. Suppose we want to divide a line segment into three parts of equal length, we have to locate points suitably to effect a trisection of the segment.

\(AM = MB\) (Fig. 5.29)     \(AP = PQ = QB\) (Fig. 5.30)

For a given line segment, there are two points of trisection. The method of obtaining this is similar to that of what we did in the case of locating the point of bisection (i.e., the mid-point). Observe the given Fig. 5.31. Here \(P\) and \(Q\) are the points of trisection of the line segment \(AB\) where \(A\) is \((x_1, y_1)\) and \(B\) is \((x_2, y_2)\). Clearly we know that, P is the mid-point of AQ and Q is the mid-point of PB. Now consider the \(\triangle ACQ\) and \(\triangle PDB\) (Also, can be verified using similarity property of triangles which will be dealt in detail in higher classes).

\[A'P' = P'Q' = Q'B'\]

If \(P\) is \((a, b)\), then

\(a = OP' = OA' + A'P'\)

\(= x_1 + \dfrac{x_2 - x_1}{3} = \dfrac{x_2 + 2x_1}{3}\);

\(b = PP' = OA'' + A''P''\)

\(= y_1 + \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{3} = \dfrac{y_2 + 2y_1}{3}\)

Thus we get the point \(P\) as \(\left(\dfrac{x_2 + 2x_1}{3}, \dfrac{y_2 + 2y_1}{3}\right)\)

If \(Q\) is \((c, d)\), then

\(c = OQ' = OB' - Q'B'\)

\(= x_2 - \left(\dfrac{x_2 - x_1}{3}\right) = \dfrac{2x_2 + x_1}{3}\);

\(d = OQ'' = OB'' - Q''B''\)

\(= y_2 - \left(\dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{3}\right) = \dfrac{2y_2 + y_1}{3}\)

Thus the required point \(Q\) is \(\left(\dfrac{2x_2 + x_1}{3}, \dfrac{2y_2 + y_1}{3}\right)\)

Example 5.16

Find the points of trisection of the line segment joining \((-2, -1)\) and \((4, 8)\).

Solution

Let \(A(-2, -1)\) and \(B(4, 8)\) are the given points.

Let \(P(a, b)\) and \(Q(c, d)\) be the points of trisection of \(AB\), so that \(AP = PQ = QB\).

By the formula proved above,

\(P\) is the point

\(\left(\dfrac{x_2 + 2x_1}{3}, \dfrac{y_2 + 2y_1}{3}\right) = \left(\dfrac{4 + 2(-2)}{3}, \dfrac{8 + 2(-1)}{3}\right)\)

\(= \left(\dfrac{4 - 4}{3}, \dfrac{8 - 2}{3}\right) = (0, 2)\)

\(Q\) is the point

\(\left(\dfrac{2x_2 + x_1}{3}, \dfrac{2y_2 + y_1}{3}\right) = \left(\dfrac{2(4) - 2}{3}, \dfrac{2(8) - 1}{3}\right)\)

\(= \left(\dfrac{8 - 2}{3}, \dfrac{16 - 1}{3}\right) = (2, 5)\)
