3.8.3 Consistency and Inconsistency of Linear Equations in Two Variables

Textbook pages 54–56 · 7 figures

Consider linear equations in two variables say

\(a x+by +c= 0 ...(1)\)

\(a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0\) ...(2)

where \(a_1, a_2, b_1, b_2, c_1\) and \(c_2\) are real numbers. Then the system has:

  1. a unique solution if \(\frac{a_1}{a_2} \ne \frac{b_1}{b_2}\) (Consistent)
  2. an Infinite number of solutions if \(\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}\) (Consistent)
  3. no solution if \(\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \ne \frac{c_1}{c_2}\) (Inconsistent)
