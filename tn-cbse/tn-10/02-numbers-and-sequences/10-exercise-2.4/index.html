Chapter 2 · Numbers and Sequences

Exercise 2.4

Textbook pages 20–20

  1. Find the next three terms of the following sequence.
    1. \(8, 24, 72, \ldots\)
    2. \(5, 1, -3, \ldots\)
    3. \(\dfrac{1}{4}, \dfrac{2}{9}, \dfrac{3}{16}, \ldots\)
  2. Find the first four terms of the sequences whose \(n^{th}\) terms are given by
    1. \(a_n = n^3 - 2\)
    2. \(a_n = (-1)^{n+1} n(n+1)\)
    3. \(a_n = 2n^2 - 6\)
  3. Find the \(n^{th}\) term of the following sequences
    1. \(2, 5, 10, 17, \ldots\)
    2. \(0, \dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{2}{3}, \ldots\)
    3. \(3, 8, 13, 18, \ldots\)
  4. Find the indicated terms of the sequences whose \(n^{th}\) terms are given by
    1. \(a_n = \dfrac{5n}{n+2};\ a_6\) and \(a_{13}\)
    2. \(a_n = -(n^2 - 4);\ a_4\) and \(a_{11}\)
  5. Find \(a_8\) and \(a_{15}\) whose \(n^{th}\) term is \(a_n = \begin{cases} \dfrac{n^2 - 1}{n + 3}; & n \text{ is even},\ n \in \mathbb{N} \\[2mm] \dfrac{n^2}{2n + 1}; & n \text{ is odd},\ n \in \mathbb{N} \end{cases}\)
  6. If \(a_1 = 1,\ a_2 = 1\) and \(a_n = 2a_{n-1} + a_{n-2},\ n \ge 3,\ n \in \mathbb{N}\), then find the first six terms of the sequence.
