4.2 Basic Elements of a Triangle

Textbook pages 2 · 3 figures

Mark 3 points A, B, C on a paper, such that they do not lie on a same straight line. Consider the line segments AB, BC and CA.

We see that they form a triangle ABC which is represented as \(\triangle ABC\) or \(\triangle BCA\) or \(\triangle CAB\). In \(\triangle ABC\), the line segments AB, BC and CA are called the sides of the triangle and \(\angle CAB\), \(\angle ABC\) and \(\angle BCA\) ( \(\angle A\), \(\angle B\) & \(\angle\) C) are called the angles of the triangle. The point of intersection of two sides of the triangle is called the vertex. A, B and C are three vertices of \(\triangle ABC\). Hence, a triangle has 3 sides, 3 angles and 3 vertices.
