Exercise 6.2

Textbook pages 25–26 · 5 figures

Think and Reflect

What procedure would you use to square a given triangle? Here, the task is to construct a square whose area is equal to the area of some given triangle. Think carefully. How would you proceed?

  1. Find the area of triangle ADE in Fig. 6.31.
  1. The parallel sides of a trapezium are 40 cm and 20 cm. If its non-parallel sides are both equal, each being 26 cm, find the area of the trapezium.
  2. Find the area of a triangle, given that its sides are 8 cm and 11 cm long, and its perimeter is 32 cm.
  3. The sides of a triangular plot are in the ratio 3: 5: 7; its perimeter is 300 m. Find its area.
  4. One diagonal of a rhombus is twice as long as the other diagonal. If the rhombus has area 128 cm , find the length of the shorter diagonal.
  5. ABCD is a parallelogram. P and Q are any two points on side AB. What can you say about the ratio area ( \(\triangle\) PCD): area ( \(\triangle\) QCD)?
  6. O is any point on the diagonal PR of a parallelogram PQRS. Prove that the areas of triangles PSO and PQO are equal.
  7. If the mid-points of the sides of a 4-gon (also known as a quadrilateral, but we prefer to call it a ‘4-gon’) are joined in order, prove that the area of the parallelogram thus formed will be half of the area of the given 4-gon. (You may wonder whether the 4-gon thus formed is always a parallelogram, and if so, why? These questions will be tackled and answered in the chapter on quadrilaterals.)
  8. In \(\triangle ABC\), the midpoint of BC is D (Fig. 6.32). Median AD is drawn. P is any point on AD. Show that area ( \(\triangle\) ABP) = area ( \(\triangle\) ACP).
  1. Given a square ABCD, let P be a point within it. Join PA, PB, PC, PD (Fig. 6.33). What is the ratio of the areas of the red region ( \(\triangle PAB\) and \(\triangle\) PCD) and the green region ( \(\triangle PBC\) and \(\triangle\) PDA)?
  2. In \(\triangle ABC\), D is the midpoint of AB. P is any point on BC, and Q is a point on AB such that CQ || PD. PQ is joined (Fig. 6.34). Prove that Area ( \(\triangle\) BPQ) \(= \frac{1}{2}\) Area ( \(\triangle\) ABC).
