Note that the algebraic expressions in Example 1 and Example 2 involve two variables, whereas the algebraic expression in Example 3 involves only one variable.

Expressions such as 4x, \(x + 1\), \(2y - 5\), \(5y + y + 2y - 1\), \(3z + 7\) are algebraic expressions that involve only one variable: x, y or z. In this chapter, we will restrict our discussion to algebraic expressions involving only one variable. You may have noticed that in an algebraic expression, the powers of a variable also appear. For

example, in the expression \(x + 5x + 1\), the highest power of x is 2,

whereas in the expression \(5y + y - 8\), the highest power of the variable

y is 3. Further, in the expression \(5y + y + 2y - 1\), the coefficient of y

is 5, that of y is 1, that of y is 2 and the constant term is \(- 1\). Such algebraic expressions involving one variable and its powers are called one-variable polynomials, univariate polynomials, or when the context is clear, simply polynomials. (‘univariate’ means ‘having one variable’). The highest power of the variable in a polynomial is called its degree. For example:

  • (i)\(5y + y + 2y - 1\) is a polynomial of degree 3. Such polynomials are

called cubic polynomials.

  • (ii)x \(+ 5x + 1\) is a polynomial of degree 2. Such polynomials are called

quadratic polynomials.

  • (iii)3z \(+ 7\) is a polynomial of degree 1. Such polynomials are called

linear polynomials.

  • (iv)The constant 8 is a polynomial of degree 0 as it can be written as

8x in which the power of the variable x is 0. Such polynomials are called constant polynomials.
