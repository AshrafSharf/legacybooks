Primes through the Ages

Prime numbers are the building blocks of all whole numbers. Starting from the time of the Greek civilisation (more than 2000 years ago) to this day, mathematicians are still struggling to uncover their secrets! Food for thought: is there a largest prime number? Or does the list of prime numbers go on without an end? A mathematician named Euclid found the answer and so will you in a later class! Fun fact: The largest prime number that anyone has ‘written down’ is so large that it would take around 6500 pages to write it! So they could only write it on a computer!

  • 6.The numbers 13 and 31 are prime numbers. Both these numbers have

  • 7.Find seven consecutive composite numbers between 1 and 100.
  • 8.Twin primes are pairs of primes having a difference of 2. For

example, 3 and 5 are twin primes. So are 17 and 19. Find the other twin primes between 1 and 100.

  • 9.Identify whether each statement is true or false. Explain.
  • a.There is no prime number whose units digit is 4.
  • b.A product of primes can also be prime.
  • c.Prime numbers do not have any factors.
  • d.All even numbers are composite numbers.
  • e.2 is a prime and so is the next number, 3. For every other

prime, the next number is composite.

  • 10.Which of the following numbers is the product of exactly three

  • 11.How many three-digit prime numbers can you make using each

of 2, 4 and 5 once?
