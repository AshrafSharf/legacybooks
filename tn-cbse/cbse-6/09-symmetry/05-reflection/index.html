Chapter 9 · Symmetry

Reflection

Textbook pages 5–6 · 3 figures

So far we have been saying that when we fold a figure along a line of symmetry, the two parts overlap completely. We could also say that the part of the figure on one side of the line of symmetry is reflected by the line to the other side; similarly, the part of the figure on the other side of the line of symmetry is reflected to the first side! Let us understand this by labeling some points on the figure.

The figure shows a square with its corners labeled A, B, C and D. Let us first consider the vertical line of symmetry. When we reflect the square along this line, the points B, C on the right get reflected to the left side and occupy the positions occupied earlier by A, D. What happens to the points A, D? A occupies the position occupied by B and D that of C!

What if we reflect along the diagonal from A to C? Where do points A, B, C and D go? What if we reflect along the horizontal line of symmetry?

A figure that has a line or lines of symmetry is thus, also said to have reflection symmetry.
