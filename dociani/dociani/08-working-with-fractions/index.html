8-1 Defining Algebraic Fractions

Any indicated quotient of two algebraic expressions is called an algebraic fraction. Examples include:

$$\frac{2}{3}, \quad \frac{5}{y}, \quad \frac{x^2 + 3x + 2}{5x - 10}$$
Definition — Algebraic Fraction
An expression of the form \(\dfrac{P}{Q}\), where \(P\) and \(Q\) are algebraic expressions and \(Q \neq 0\), is an algebraic fraction. The fraction is defined only when its denominator is not zero.

To find the values that must be excluded from the replacement set of the variable, set the denominator equal to zero and solve.

Key Insight
Before performing any operation with algebraic fractions, always determine the values that make the denominator zero. These values are excluded from the domain.
FractionExcluded Value(s)Reason
\(\dfrac{x}{x-2}\)\(x = 2\)\(x - 2 = 0\)
\(\dfrac{3}{x^2 - 9}\)\(x = 3, \; x = -3\)\((x-3)(x+3) = 0\)
\(\dfrac{5}{2x+1}\)\(x = -\tfrac{1}{2}\)\(2x + 1 = 0\)

8-2 Reducing Fractions

Multiplication Property of Fractions
Dividing or multiplying the numerator and denominator of a fraction by the same nonzero number produces a fraction equal to the given one: $$\frac{ac}{bc} = \frac{a}{b}, \quad \text{provided } c \neq 0$$

A fraction is in lowest terms when its numerator and denominator have no common factor other than \(1\) and \(-1\). Reducing to lowest terms means dividing the numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor.

Figure 8.1 — Reducing a fraction: divide numerator and denominator by their GCF
Caution
You can reduce a fraction only when the numerator and denominator share a common factor. You cannot cancel terms that are merely added or subtracted: $$\frac{x + 3}{3} \neq x \qquad \text{(Wrong! The 3 is a term, not a factor.)}$$

8-3 Multiplying Fractions

Rule for Multiplying Fractions
The product of two fractions is a fraction whose numerator is the product of the numerators and whose denominator is the product of the denominators: $$\frac{x}{c} \cdot \frac{y}{d} = \frac{xy}{cd} \qquad (c \neq 0,\; d \neq 0)$$

You can simplify the multiplication by first factoring numerators and denominators, then cancelling common factors before multiplying.

Example
$$\frac{3x-3}{5x+2} \cdot \frac{25x^2 - 4}{x^2 - 2x + 1}$$ Factor each part: $$= \frac{3(x-1)}{5x+2} \cdot \frac{(5x-2)(5x+2)}{(x-1)(x-1)}$$ Cancel common factors \((5x+2)\) and \((x-1)\): $$= \frac{3(5x-2)}{x-1} = \frac{15x - 6}{x - 1}$$

8-4 Dividing Fractions

Rule for Dividing Fractions
To divide fractions, multiply the dividend by the reciprocal of the divisor: $$\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{d}{c} = \frac{ad}{bc} \qquad (b,c,d \neq 0)$$

Remember that the reciprocal of \(\dfrac{c}{d}\) is \(\dfrac{d}{c}\), provided \(c \neq 0\) and \(d \neq 0\).

8-5 / 8-6 Least Common Denominator & Adding Fractions

Finding the L.C.D.

To find the lowest common denominator of several fractions:

  1. Factor each denominator completely.
  2. Form the product of each different factor, taken the greatest number of times it appears in any single denominator.
Key Insight — Prime Factorization Method

To find the L.C.D. of \(12\) and \(90\):

$$12 = 2^2 \cdot 3, \qquad 90 = 2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5$$ $$\text{L.C.D.} = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 = 180$$

Adding and Subtracting Fractions

Adding Fractions with Equal Denominators
$$\frac{a}{d} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a + c}{d} \qquad (d \neq 0)$$

For fractions with unequal denominators, first rewrite each fraction with the L.C.D. as the denominator, then add the numerators.

8-7 Mixed Expressions

A mixed expression is the sum or difference of a polynomial and a fraction. Like a mixed numeral (e.g., \(2\tfrac{3}{8}\)), it can be written as a single fraction:

$$x + \frac{3}{x} = \frac{x^2}{x} + \frac{3}{x} = \frac{x^2 + 3}{x}$$

Conversely, if the numerator is a polynomial, you can change a fraction to a mixed expression by performing the division:

$$\frac{2c^2 + 5c - 6}{c - 1} = 2c + 7 + \frac{1}{c - 1}$$

8-8 Solving Equations with Fractional Coefficients

When an equation has fractions, the standard strategy is to multiply both sides by the L.C.D. to clear all fractions.

Strategy: Clearing Fractions
  1. Find the L.C.D. of all fractional terms.
  2. Multiply every term of the equation by the L.C.D.
  3. Solve the resulting equation (no fractions!).
  4. Check: substitute back into the original equation.
Caution — Extraneous Roots
When the variable appears in a denominator (a fractional equation), multiplying by an expression that can equal zero may introduce extraneous roots. Always check your answers in the original equation. Only values producing true statements belong to the solution set.

8-9 Ratios

Definition — Ratio
A ratio of one number to another is the quotient of the first number divided by the second. The ratio of \(a\) to \(b\) can be written as \(a \div b\), \(a:b\), or \(\dfrac{a}{b}\).

When comparing measurements of the same kind, first express both in the same unit, then compute the quotient. For example, to compare a 2-foot line to a 4-inch line, convert 2 feet to 24 inches, giving the ratio \(\dfrac{24}{4} = 6:1\).

By the multiplication property of fractions, the ratio \(5:4\) compares not only \(5\) and \(4\), but also \(10\) and \(8\), \(15\) and \(12\), and in general \(5n\) and \(4n\) for any nonzero \(n\).

8-10 Per Cent Problems

The ratio of one number to another is often expressed as a per cent. The words "per cent" (\(\%\)) mean divided by 100 or hundredths:

$$4\% = \frac{4}{100} = 0.04, \qquad 125\% = \frac{125}{100} = 1.25, \qquad 100\% = 1$$
The Percentage Relationship
$$\frac{\text{percentage}}{\text{base}} = \text{rate} \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad p = r \cdot b \quad (b \neq 0)$$ where percentage (\(p\)) is a number equal to a per cent (rate, \(r\)) of another number called the base (\(b\)).

8-11 Problems about Work

Work Rate Principle
If a job takes \(n\) units of time to complete, then the rate of work is \(\dfrac{1}{n}\) of the job per unit of time. When two workers (or machines) work together, their combined rate is the sum of their individual rates: $$\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} = \frac{1}{t}$$ where \(a\) and \(b\) are the individual times and \(t\) is the time working together.

8-12 Motion Problems in Two Parts

Many motion problems involve travel with and against a current or wind. The key relationships are:

Distance–Rate–Time with Current/Wind
$$\text{distance} = \text{rate} \times \text{time} \qquad (d = rt)$$
  • With the current/wind: effective rate \(= r + c\)
  • Against the current/wind: effective rate \(= r - c\)
where \(r\) is the speed in still conditions and \(c\) is the speed of the current or wind.

When the time is the same for both legs of a trip, you set the time expressions equal to each other:

$$\frac{d_1}{r + c} = \frac{d_2}{r - c}$$

Worked Problems

Worked Problem 1 — Reducing an Algebraic Fraction
Reduce to lowest terms: \(\;\dfrac{15b + 5}{6b^2 - b - 1}\)
Solution
  1. Factor the numerator: \(15b + 5 = 5(3b + 1)\).
  2. Factor the denominator: \(6b^2 - b - 1 = (2b - 1)(3b + 1)\).
  3. Divide numerator and denominator by the common factor \((3b + 1)\): $$\frac{5(3b+1)}{(2b-1)(3b+1)} = \frac{5}{2b - 1}$$

Answer: \(\;\dfrac{5}{2b - 1}\), provided \(b \neq \tfrac{1}{2}\) and \(b \neq -\tfrac{1}{3}\).

Worked Problem 2 — Multiplying Rational Expressions
Simplify: \(\;\dfrac{3x - 3}{5x + 2} \cdot \dfrac{25x^2 - 4}{x^2 - 2x + 1}\)
Solution
  1. Factor each expression: $$\frac{3(x-1)}{5x+2} \cdot \frac{(5x-2)(5x+2)}{(x-1)(x-1)}$$
  2. Cancel common factors \((5x+2)\) and one \((x-1)\): $$= \frac{3(5x-2)}{x - 1}$$

Answer: \(\;\dfrac{15x - 6}{x - 1}\), for \(x \neq 1, \; x \neq -\tfrac{2}{5}\).

Worked Problem 3 — Adding Fractions with Unequal Denominators
Simplify: \(\;\dfrac{a+1}{a^2 - 2a - 3} - \dfrac{1}{a^2 + a} - \dfrac{3}{a^2 - 3a}\)
Solution
  1. Factor each denominator:
    • \(a^2 - 2a - 3 = (a - 3)(a + 1)\)
    • \(a^2 + a = a(a + 1)\)
    • \(a^2 - 3a = a(a - 3)\)
  2. The L.C.D. is \(a(a + 1)(a - 3)\).
  3. Rewrite each fraction with the L.C.D.: $$\frac{(a+1) \cdot a}{a(a-3)(a+1)} - \frac{1 \cdot (a-3)}{a(a+1)(a-3)} - \frac{3(a+1)}{a(a-3)(a+1)}$$
  4. Combine and simplify the numerator: $$\frac{a^2 + a - a + 3 - 3a - 3}{a(a+1)(a-3)} = \frac{a^2 - 3a}{a(a+1)(a-3)}$$
  5. Factor: \(a^2 - 3a = a(a-3)\), then cancel: $$= \frac{a(a-3)}{a(a+1)(a-3)} = \frac{1}{a+1}$$

Answer: \(\;\dfrac{1}{a+1}\), for \(a \neq 0, \; a \neq 3, \; a \neq -1\).

Worked Problem 4 — Solving a Fractional Equation
Solve: \(\;\dfrac{60}{z^2 - 36} + 1 = \dfrac{5}{z - 6}\)
Solution
  1. Note \(z^2 - 36 = (z-6)(z+6)\). The L.C.D. is \((z-6)(z+6)\).
  2. Multiply every term by \((z^2 - 36)\): $$60 + z^2 - 36 = 5(z + 6)$$
  3. Simplify: \(z^2 + 24 = 5z + 30\), so \(z^2 - 5z - 6 = 0\).
  4. Factor: \((z - 6)(z + 1) = 0\), giving \(z = 6\) or \(z = -1\).
  5. Check: When \(z = 6\), the denominators \(z^2 - 36\) and \(z - 6\) equal zero — so \(z = 6\) is extraneous.
    When \(z = -1\): \(\dfrac{60}{1 - 36} + 1 = \dfrac{60}{-35} + 1 = -\dfrac{12}{7} + 1 = -\dfrac{5}{7}\). Also \(\dfrac{5}{-1-6} = -\dfrac{5}{7}\). True!

Answer: \(\;z = -1\). The value \(z = 6\) is extraneous.

Worked Problem 5 — A Work Problem
One pipe can fill a tank in 5 hours. A second pipe can fill it in 3 hours. How long will it take both pipes working together?
Solution
  1. The first pipe fills \(\dfrac{1}{5}\) of the tank per hour. The second fills \(\dfrac{1}{3}\) per hour.
  2. Let \(t\) = hours for both pipes together. Together they fill \(\dfrac{1}{5} + \dfrac{1}{3}\) per hour.
  3. Set up the equation: $$\frac{t}{5} + \frac{t}{3} = 1$$
  4. Multiply by the L.C.D. (\(15\)): $$3t + 5t = 15 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad 8t = 15 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad t = \frac{15}{8}$$

Answer: Both pipes fill the tank in \(\dfrac{15}{8} = 1\tfrac{7}{8}\) hours (1 hour 52.5 minutes).

Check: In \(\frac{15}{8}\) hours, pipe 1 fills \(\frac{15}{8} \cdot \frac{1}{5} = \frac{3}{8}\) and pipe 2 fills \(\frac{15}{8} \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \frac{5}{8}\). Together: \(\frac{3}{8} + \frac{5}{8} = 1\). ✓

Worked Problem 6 — A Motion Problem
An airplane flying at 600 mph in calm air covers 2520 miles with the wind in the same time it covers 2280 miles against the wind. Find the speed of the wind.
Solution
  1. Let \(s\) = speed of the wind (mph).
    • With wind: rate \(= 600 + s\), distance \(= 2520\)
    • Against wind: rate \(= 600 - s\), distance \(= 2280\)
  2. Since the times are equal: \(\;\dfrac{2520}{600 + s} = \dfrac{2280}{600 - s}\).
  3. Cross-multiply: $$2520(600 - s) = 2280(600 + s)$$ $$1{,}512{,}000 - 2520s = 1{,}368{,}000 + 2280s$$
  4. Solve: $$144{,}000 = 4800s \quad \Longrightarrow \quad s = 30$$

Answer: The wind speed is 30 mph.

Check: Time with wind: \(\frac{2520}{630} = 4\) hours. Time against wind: \(\frac{2280}{570} = 4\) hours. Equal! ✓
