Chapter 6

Working with Polynomials

Master the language of algebra: add, subtract, multiply, and divide polynomials using the laws of exponents, the distributive property, and systematic long division.

What You'll Learn

  • Identify monomials, binomials, trinomials and their degrees
  • Add and subtract polynomials by combining like terms
  • Apply the rule of exponents for multiplication: \(b^m \cdot b^n = b^{m+n}\)
  • Multiply monomials and raise monomials to powers
  • Multiply a polynomial by a monomial using the distributive property
  • Multiply two polynomials (including FOIL for binomials)
  • Solve area problems that require polynomial multiplication
  • Apply the rule of exponents for division: \(\frac{b^m}{b^n} = b^{m-n}\)
  • Divide a polynomial by a monomial term-by-term
  • Perform long division of polynomials

6-1 & 6-2 Adding and Subtracting Polynomials

Definition — Polynomial Vocabulary

A monomial is a single term: a numeral, a variable, or a product of a numeral and one or more variables (e.g., \(7\), \(x\), \(-2xy\)).

A polynomial is a monomial or the indicated sum of monomials. Special names:

  • Binomial — polynomial of two terms (e.g., \(5y^2 + 7\))
  • Trinomial — polynomial of three terms (e.g., \(a^2 - 2ab + b^2\))
Definition — Degree

The degree of a monomial is the sum of the exponents of all its variables. For example, \(3x^2y^5z\) has degree \(2+5+1=8\).

The degree of a polynomial is the greatest degree among its terms. The polynomial \(5x^4 - 7x^3y^2 + 2xy\) has term-degrees 4, 5, and 2, so its degree is 5.

Adding Polynomials

To add two polynomials, use the commutative, associative, and distributive properties to combine similar (like) terms—terms with exactly the same variable factors.

Worked Example 1 — Adding Polynomials

Find the sum:  \((5x^2y + 15) + (7x^2y - 3x + 2)\)

1
Group like terms: \((5 + 7)x^2y - 3x + (15 + 2)\)
2
Simplify: \(12x^2y - 3x + 17\)

Check (let \(x=2, y=3\)):  First polynomial: \(5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 + 15 = 75\).  Second: \(7 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 - 6 + 2 = 80\).  Sum: \(75 + 80 = 155\).  Result: \(12 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 - 6 + 17 = 155\).  \(\checkmark\)

Key Insight — Checking with Evaluation

You can verify any polynomial computation by substituting small numerical values for the variables and comparing. Avoid choosing 0 or 1, since those can mask errors (any power of 1 is 1; any term with 0 vanishes).

Subtracting Polynomials

To subtract a polynomial, add the opposite of each term of the subtrahend, then combine like terms.

Worked Example 2 — Subtracting Polynomials

Simplify:  \((5z + 6) - (2z - 8)\)

1
Distribute the negative: \(5z + 6 - 2z + 8\)
2
Combine like terms: \(3z + 14\)
Key Insight — Arranging Terms

When working with polynomials, arrange terms in order of decreasing degree (or increasing degree) in a chosen variable. For example: \(a^3 + 6a^2 + 12a + 8\).

6-3 The Product of Powers

Since \(b^4 = b \cdot b \cdot b \cdot b\), the product of two powers of the same base can be found by counting factors:

$$b^4 \cdot b^2 = \underbrace{(b \cdot b \cdot b \cdot b)}_{4\text{ factors}} \cdot \underbrace{(b \cdot b)}_{2\text{ factors}} = b^{4+2} = b^6$$
Rule of Exponents for Multiplication

For all positive integers \(m\) and \(n\):

$$b^m \cdot b^n = b^{m+n}$$

The bases must be the same. You cannot simplify \(x^3 \cdot y^2\) this way because the bases differ.

To multiply monomials, multiply the numerical coefficients and apply the exponent rule to each matching variable factor:

$$(2r^3)(-3r^2z^5) = (2 \cdot -3)(r^{3+2})(z^5) = -6r^5z^5$$

6-4 Powers of Monomials

Rule of Exponents — Power of a Product

For every positive integer \(m\):

$$(ab)^m = a^m \, b^m$$

Example:  \((-3x)^4 = (-3)^4 \cdot x^4 = 81x^4\)

Rule of Exponents — Power of a Power

For all positive integers \(m\) and \(n\):

$$(b^m)^n = b^{mn}$$

Example:  \((b^2)^3 = b^{2 \cdot 3} = b^6\)

Both rules combine naturally:

$$(-7h^7v^3)^3 = (-7)^3 (h^7)^3 (v^3)^3 = -343\,h^{21}v^9$$
Caution — Sign in Even vs. Odd Powers

A negative base raised to an even power yields a positive result: \((-3)^4 = 81\). A negative base raised to an odd power stays negative: \((-3)^3 = -27\). Watch the parentheses: \(-3^4 = -(3^4) = -81\), but \((-3)^4 = 81\).

6-5 Multiplying a Polynomial by a Monomial

The distributive property extends naturally: multiply each term of the polynomial by the monomial, then add the products.

Distributive Property
$$a(b + c + d) = ab + ac + ad$$
Worked Example 3 — Monomial Times Polynomial

Find the product:  \(-7y^2(3y^3 - 2y^2 + 1)\)

1
Multiply each term: \((-7y^2)(3y^3) + (-7y^2)(-2y^2) + (-7y^2)(1)\)
2
Apply exponent rules: \(-21y^5 + 14y^4 - 7y^2\)

6-6 Multiplying Two Polynomials

To multiply one polynomial by another, use the distributive property: multiply each term of one polynomial by each term of the other, then combine like terms.

Worked Example 4 — Multiplying Two Binomials

Find the product:  \((2x + 3)(4x + 5)\)

1
Apply distributive property: \((2x)(4x + 5) + 3(4x + 5)\)
2
Multiply each pair: \(8x^2 + 10x + 12x + 15\)
3
Combine like terms: \(8x^2 + 22x + 15\)
The area model shows each partial product as a sub-rectangle. Combine like terms (10x + 12x) to get the final result.
Key Insight — The Vertical Format

You can also multiply vertically, working left to right, just like arithmetic long multiplication:

    4x + 5
×  2x + 3
  8x² + 10x      ← 2x(4x + 5)
         12x + 15 ← 3(4x + 5)
  8x² + 22x + 15

Special Products

Several patterns emerge from binomial multiplication and are worth memorizing:

Important Identities
  • Square of a sum:  \((x + y)^2 = x^2 + 2xy + y^2\)
  • Square of a difference:  \((x - y)^2 = x^2 - 2xy + y^2\)
  • Difference of squares:  \((x + y)(x - y) = x^2 - y^2\)

6-7 Problems about Areas

Area problems give you a real-world context for polynomial multiplication. A sketch is almost always the key to setting up the equation correctly.

Worked Example 5 — Foundation Area Problem

Problem: A building foundation is rectangular with 2-foot-thick concrete walls. The inside length is twice the inside width. The area of the concrete (top face) is 496 square feet. Find the inside dimensions.

1
Let \(w\) = inside width (ft). Then inside length \(= 2w\).
2
Outer dimensions: width \(= w + 4\), length \(= 2w + 4\) (add 2 ft on each side).
3
Area of concrete = Total area − Inner area: $$(2w + 4)(w + 4) - (2w)(w) = 496$$
4
Expand: \(2w^2 + 8w + 4w + 16 - 2w^2 = 496\), so \(12w + 16 = 496\).
5
Solve: \(12w = 480\), hence \(w = 40\) and \(2w = 80\).

Answer: Inside width = 40 ft, inside length = 80 ft.

Check: Outer area \(= 84 \times 44 = 3696\). Inner area \(= 80 \times 40 = 3200\). Concrete area \(= 3696 - 3200 = 496\).  \(\checkmark\)

6-8 Powers of Polynomials

The area of a square with side \(s\) is \(s^2\); the volume of a cube with edge \(s\) is \(s^3\). When \(s\) is itself a polynomial, to expand means to express the power as a sum of monomials.

Worked Example 6 — Expanding \((2x - 3)^2\)

Expand:  \((2x - 3)^2 = (2x - 3)(2x - 3)\)

1
Apply the identity \((a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2\) with \(a = 2x\), \(b = 3\):
2
\((2x)^2 - 2(2x)(3) + 3^2 = 4x^2 - 12x + 9\)

6-9 Laws of Exponents for Division

Division of powers with the same base follows naturally from multiplication:

Rule of Exponents for Division

For positive integers \(m\) and \(n\) and \(b \neq 0\):

  • If \(m > n\):  \(\displaystyle\frac{b^m}{b^n} = b^{m-n}\)
  • If \(m < n\):  \(\displaystyle\frac{b^m}{b^n} = \frac{1}{b^{n-m}}\)
  • If \(m = n\):  \(\displaystyle\frac{b^m}{b^n} = 1\)  (equivalently, \(b^0 = 1\) for \(b \neq 0\))
Property of Quotients

For nonzero \(c\) and \(d\):

$$\frac{xy}{cd} = \frac{x}{c} \cdot \frac{y}{d}$$

This lets you divide the numerical and variable parts of a monomial separately.

Example:

$$\frac{-40d^5e}{-5de^3} = \frac{-40}{-5} \cdot \frac{d^5}{d} \cdot \frac{e}{e^3} = 8d^4 \cdot \frac{1}{e^2} = \frac{8d^4}{e^2}$$

6-10 Dividing a Polynomial by a Monomial

Just as \(\frac{93 + 48}{3} = \frac{93}{3} + \frac{48}{3} = 31 + 16 = 47\), you divide each term of the polynomial by the monomial:

$$\frac{ax + ay}{a} = \frac{ax}{a} + \frac{ay}{a} = x + y$$
Worked Example 7 — Dividing by a Monomial

Simplify:  \(\displaystyle\frac{12a^3 - 3a}{3a}\)

1
Divide each term: \(\displaystyle\frac{12a^3}{3a} - \frac{3a}{3a}\)
2
Simplify: \(4a^2 - 1\)

6-11 Dividing a Polynomial by a Polynomial

Polynomial long division follows the same pattern as arithmetic long division: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down, and repeat.

Worked Example 8 — Polynomial Long Division

Divide:  \((x^2 + 5x + 6) \div (x + 2)\)

1
Divide leading terms: \(\frac{x^2}{x} = x\). Multiply: \(x(x+2) = x^2 + 2x\).
2
Subtract: \((x^2 + 5x + 6) - (x^2 + 2x) = 3x + 6\).
3
Divide leading terms: \(\frac{3x}{x} = 3\). Multiply: \(3(x+2) = 3x + 6\).
4
Subtract: \((3x + 6) - (3x + 6) = 0\). Remainder is 0.

Answer:  \(x + 3\)

Check:  \((x + 2)(x + 3) = x^2 + 5x + 6\).  \(\checkmark\)

Key Insight — Missing Terms

When a power of the variable is missing from the dividend, insert it with coefficient 0 as a placeholder. For example, to divide \(m^3 - 7\) by \(m^2 + 2m + 1\), write the dividend as \(m^3 + 0m^2 + 0m - 7\).

General Division Relationship
$$\frac{\text{Dividend}}{\text{Divisor}} = \text{Quotient} + \frac{\text{Remainder}}{\text{Divisor}}$$

The process stops when the remainder is 0, or when its degree is less than the degree of the divisor.

Practice Problems

Practice 1 — Combining Like Terms

Simplify:  \((3x^3 - 5x^2 + 8) + (-x - 10) + (2x^2 - 4x^3 + x)\)

Show Solution

Group like terms: \((3 - 4)x^3 + (-5 + 2)x^2 + (-1 + 1)x + (8 - 10)\)

\(= -x^3 - 3x^2 + 0x - 2 = -x^3 - 3x^2 - 2\)

Practice 2 — Exponent Rules

Simplify:  \((-2a^3b^2)^3 \cdot (5a^2b)\)

Show Solution

\((-2)^3(a^3)^3(b^2)^3 \cdot 5a^2b = -8a^9b^6 \cdot 5a^2b\)

\(= -40a^{11}b^7\)

Practice 3 — Multiplying Polynomials

Find the product:  \((3y - 4)(4y - 6)\)

Show Solution

\(3y \cdot 4y + 3y \cdot (-6) + (-4) \cdot 4y + (-4)(-6)\)

\(= 12y^2 - 18y - 16y + 24 = 12y^2 - 34y + 24\)

Practice 4 — Area Problem

A square garden is bordered by a path that is 2 feet wide. The area of the path is 184 square feet. How long is a side of the garden?

Show Solution

Let \(s\) = side of the garden. Outer square has side \(s + 4\).

Path area: \((s + 4)^2 - s^2 = 184\)

\(s^2 + 8s + 16 - s^2 = 184\)

\(8s + 16 = 184\), so \(8s = 168\), hence \(s = 21\) feet.

Practice 5 — Polynomial Long Division

Divide:  \((4n^2 - 29n + 45) \div (n - 5)\)

Show Solution

\(\frac{4n^2}{n} = 4n\).  Multiply: \(4n(n-5) = 4n^2 - 20n\).  Subtract: \(-9n + 45\).

\(\frac{-9n}{n} = -9\).  Multiply: \(-9(n-5) = -9n + 45\).  Subtract: \(0\).

Quotient: \(4n - 9\)

Check: \((n-5)(4n-9) = 4n^2 - 9n - 20n + 45 = 4n^2 - 29n + 45\). \(\checkmark\)

Practice 6 — Division with Remainder

Divide:  \((2x^3 + 3x^2 - 5x + 7) \div (x + 3)\)

Show Solution

\(\frac{2x^3}{x} = 2x^2\). Multiply \(2x^2(x+3) = 2x^3 + 6x^2\). Subtract: \(-3x^2 - 5x + 7\).

\(\frac{-3x^2}{x} = -3x\). Multiply \(-3x(x+3) = -3x^2 - 9x\). Subtract: \(4x + 7\).

\(\frac{4x}{x} = 4\). Multiply \(4(x+3) = 4x + 12\). Subtract: \(-5\).

Answer: \(2x^2 - 3x + 4 + \dfrac{-5}{x + 3}\)

\(b^0 = 1\)  (for \(b \neq 0\))
