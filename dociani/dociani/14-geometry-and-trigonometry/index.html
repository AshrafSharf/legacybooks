14-1 Angles and Triangles

This chapter marks a shift from the purely algebraic to the geometric. The study of geometry examines the properties of points, lines, planes, triangles, circles, and other figures made up of points. Just as algebra studies the properties of sets of numbers, geometry studies properties of sets of points.

Definition — Ray

If a point \(P\) with coordinate \(p\) is taken on a line, then the set of points consisting of \(P\) and all points whose coordinates equal or exceed \(p\) is called a ray proceeding from \(P\). The point \(P\) is called the endpoint of the ray.

Definition — Angle

An angle is a set of points consisting of two rays proceeding from the same point. The point is called the vertex, and the rays are called the sides of the angle. If the union of the two rays is a line, the angle is called a straight angle (measure 180°).

Types of Angles

  • Acute angle: measure greater than 0° and less than 90°
  • Right angle: measure exactly 90°
  • Obtuse angle: measure greater than 90° and less than 180°
  • Straight angle: measure exactly 180°
Definition — Triangle

If \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\) are three noncollinear points, the set of points comprising the segments \(\overline{AB}\), \(\overline{BC}\), and \(\overline{AC}\) is a triangle. If one angle is a right angle, the triangle is a right triangle; the side opposite the right angle is called the hypotenuse.

Theorem — Angle Sum Property

The sum of the measures of the angles of any triangle is \(180°\).

$$m\angle A + m\angle B + m\angle C = 180°$$
Key Insight

A triangle can have at most one right angle or obtuse angle, because the measures of the three angles must sum to 180°. This means at least two angles in every triangle are acute.

14-2 The Pythagorean Theorem

One of the most important results in all of mathematics is the Pythagorean Theorem, which relates the three sides of a right triangle.

Pythagorean Theorem

In any right triangle, the square of the length of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the lengths of the other two sides:

$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$

where \(c\) is the hypotenuse, and \(a\) and \(b\) are the legs.

Figure 14-1. A right triangle with legs \(a\) and \(b\), and hypotenuse \(c\).
Key Insight — Converse

The converse is also true: if a triangle has sides of length \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) with \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\), then the triangle is a right triangle with the right angle opposite the side of length \(c\).

You can use the Pythagorean Theorem to determine whether a triangle is a right triangle. For example, a triangle with sides 5, 12, and 13 is a right triangle because \(5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13^2\). But a triangle with sides 1, 2, and 3 is not (and in fact cannot even form a triangle, since \(1 + 2 = 3\)).

14-3 Special Right Triangles

Two families of right triangles appear so frequently in mathematics that their side ratios should be memorized.

The 45-45-90 Triangle

When a right triangle has two equal legs, both acute angles measure 45°. If each leg has length \(s\), then by the Pythagorean Theorem the hypotenuse is:

$$c = \sqrt{s^2 + s^2} = \sqrt{2s^2} = s\sqrt{2}$$
45-45-90 Side Ratios

The sides are in the ratio \(\;1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}\).

If each leg is \(s\), then: Leg = \(s\),   Leg = \(s\),   Hypotenuse = \(s\sqrt{2}\).

Figure 14-2. The 45-45-90 triangle with side ratio \(1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}\).

The 30-60-90 Triangle

Consider an equilateral triangle with side length \(2s\). Drawing an altitude from one vertex to the opposite side bisects that side and creates two congruent 30-60-90 right triangles. The shorter leg (opposite 30°) is \(s\), the hypotenuse is \(2s\), and the longer leg (opposite 60°) is:

$$b = \sqrt{(2s)^2 - s^2} = \sqrt{4s^2 - s^2} = \sqrt{3s^2} = s\sqrt{3}$$
30-60-90 Side Ratios

The sides are in the ratio \(\;1 : \sqrt{3} : 2\).

If the shorter leg is \(s\), then: Short leg = \(s\),   Long leg = \(s\sqrt{3}\),   Hypotenuse = \(2s\).

Figure 14-3. The 30-60-90 triangle with side ratio \(1 : \sqrt{3} : 2\).
Caution

In the 30-60-90 triangle, the shorter leg is always opposite the 30° angle and the longer leg is always opposite the 60° angle. Mixing them up is a common error. Remember: the larger the angle, the longer the opposite side.

14-4 Similar Triangles

Two triangles are similar if they have the same shape, though not necessarily the same size. This means their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are proportional.

Definition — Similar Triangles

Triangles \(\triangle ABC\) and \(\triangle DEF\) are similar (written \(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF\)) if their corresponding angles are congruent and their corresponding sides are in proportion:

$$\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF} = \frac{AC}{DF}$$
AA Similarity Principle

If two angles of one triangle are equal in measure to two angles of another triangle, then the triangles are similar. (Since the angle sum is always 180°, the third angles must also be equal.)

Key Insight

All 30-60-90 triangles are similar to each other, and all 45-45-90 triangles are similar to each other. That is why the side ratios given above work for every triangle of that type, regardless of its actual size. Similarity is the bridge between angle measurement and side measurement.

14-5 Trigonometric Ratios

The word trigonometry means "triangle measure." The trigonometric functions assign a real number to each angle by using the coordinates of points on the unit circle (a circle of radius 1 centered at the origin).

The Unit-Circle Definitions

Place an angle in standard position: vertex at the origin, initial ray along the positive \(x\)-axis. The terminal ray intersects the unit circle at a point \(P(x, y)\).

Sine, Cosine, and Tangent

For an angle \(A\) in standard position, if \(P(x, y)\) is the point where the terminal ray meets the unit circle:

$$\sin A = y \qquad \cos A = x \qquad \tan A = \frac{y}{x} \;\;(x \neq 0)$$

More generally, for any point \(Q(x, y)\) on the terminal ray at distance \(r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\) from the origin:

$$\sin A = \frac{y}{r}, \qquad \cos A = \frac{x}{r}, \qquad \tan A = \frac{y}{x}$$

Right-Triangle Definitions

In any right triangle, for an acute angle \(A\):

SOH-CAH-TOA
$$\sin A = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \qquad \cos A = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \qquad \tan A = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$$
Fundamental Pythagorean Identity

For every angle \(A\):

$$\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A = 1$$

This follows directly from the Pythagorean Theorem applied to the unit circle.

Values for Special Angles

Angle \(\sin\) \(\cos\) \(\tan\)
\(0°\) \(0\) \(1\) \(0\)
\(30°\) \(\dfrac{1}{2}\) \(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\) \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\)
\(45°\) \(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\) \(\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\) \(1\)
\(60°\) \(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\) \(\dfrac{1}{2}\) \(\sqrt{3}\)
\(90°\) \(1\) \(0\) undefined
Key Insight — Relationship Between Sine and Cosine

Since \(\tan A = \frac{\sin A}{\cos A}\), you can always find the tangent if you know the sine and cosine. Also notice that \(\sin A = \cos(90° - A)\) and \(\cos A = \sin(90° - A)\): the sine and cosine are cofunctions of complementary angles.

14-6 Using Trigonometric Ratios

To solve a right triangle means to find all unknown sides and angles. By combining the Pythagorean Theorem with the sine, cosine, and tangent functions, you can solve any right triangle given sufficient information.

Strategy for Solving Right Triangles

  1. Identify the right angle and label the sides relative to the given acute angle.
  2. Choose the trigonometric ratio that involves the known side and the unknown side.
  3. Set up the equation and solve for the unknown.
  4. Use \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\) or the angle sum property to find any remaining parts.

14-7 Applications of Trigonometry

Trigonometry has wide-ranging applications in surveying, navigation, architecture, and physics. Two of the most common scenarios involve angles of elevation and angles of depression.

Definition — Angle of Elevation and Depression

The angle of elevation is the angle formed between the horizontal and the line of sight when looking upward at an object. The angle of depression is the angle formed between the horizontal and the line of sight when looking downward.

Figure 14-4. Angle of elevation \(\theta\): the observer looks up at an object of height \(h\) at horizontal distance \(d\). Then \(\tan \theta = h/d\).

Worked Problems

Problem 1 — Pythagorean Theorem

A right triangle has legs of length 5 and 12. Find the hypotenuse.

Solution
Step 1:Apply the Pythagorean Theorem.
$$c^2 = a^2 + b^2 = 5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169$$
Step 2:Take the positive square root.
$$c = \sqrt{169} = 13$$

The hypotenuse is 13. (Note: 5, 12, 13 is a Pythagorean triple.)

Problem 2 — 45-45-90 Triangle

In a right triangle \(\triangle ABC\), \(AC = BC\) and \(AB = 2\). Find \(AC\) and \(BC\).

Solution
Step 1:Since both legs are equal and \(\angle C = 90°\), this is a 45-45-90 triangle.
Step 2:Let each leg be \(s\). Then the hypotenuse is \(s\sqrt{2} = 2\).
Step 3:Solve for \(s\):
$$s = \frac{2}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{2} = \sqrt{2}$$

Both \(AC\) and \(BC\) equal \(\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414\).

Problem 3 — 30-60-90 Triangle

A 30-60-90 triangle has a hypotenuse of length 10. Find the lengths of both legs.

Solution
Step 1:In a 30-60-90 triangle, hypotenuse \(= 2s\), so \(2s = 10\) and \(s = 5\).
Step 2:The short leg (opposite 30°) is \(s = 5\).
Step 3:The long leg (opposite 60°) is \(s\sqrt{3} = 5\sqrt{3} \approx 8.66\).

The legs are 5 and \(5\sqrt{3}\).

Problem 4 — Using Trigonometric Ratios (Ladder Problem)

How long a ladder is needed to reach a window 25 feet above the ground, if the ladder makes an angle of 75° with the ground?

Solution
Step 1:The window height is the side opposite the 75° angle; the ladder is the hypotenuse.
Step 2:Use the sine ratio:
$$\sin 75° = \frac{25}{\ell}$$
Step 3:Solve for \(\ell\):
$$\ell = \frac{25}{\sin 75°} = \frac{25}{0.9659} \approx 25.9$$

You would need approximately a 26-foot ladder.

Problem 5 — Angle of Elevation

A surveyor stands 200 feet from the base of a building. The angle of elevation to the top of the building is 35°. How tall is the building?

Solution
Step 1:The height \(h\) is opposite the 35° angle; the distance 200 ft is adjacent.
Step 2:Use the tangent ratio:
$$\tan 35° = \frac{h}{200}$$
Step 3:Solve for \(h\):
$$h = 200 \cdot \tan 35° = 200 \times 0.7002 \approx 140.0$$

The building is approximately 140 feet tall.

Problem 6 — Angle of Depression

From the top of a cliff 150 meters high, the angle of depression to a boat at sea is 20°. How far is the boat from the base of the cliff?

Solution
Step 1:The angle of depression from the cliff top equals the angle of elevation from the boat (alternate interior angles).
Step 2:In the right triangle: the cliff height 150 m is opposite the 20° angle; the horizontal distance \(d\) is adjacent.
Step 3:Use the tangent ratio:
$$\tan 20° = \frac{150}{d}$$
Step 4:Solve for \(d\):
$$d = \frac{150}{\tan 20°} = \frac{150}{0.3640} \approx 412.1$$

The boat is approximately 412 meters from the base of the cliff.
