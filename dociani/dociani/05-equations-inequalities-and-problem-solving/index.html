5-1 Transforming Equations

The transformations you learned for arithmetic numbers work equally well with directed (signed) numbers. You may add, subtract, multiply, or divide each member of an equation by the same nonzero directed number to produce an equivalent equation—one with the same solution set.

Properties of Equality (Directed Numbers)

If \(a = b\), then for any directed number \(c\):

  • \(a + c = b + c\)   (addition / subtraction)
  • \(a \cdot c = b \cdot c\)   (multiplication, \(c \neq 0\))
  • \(\displaystyle \frac{a}{c} = \frac{b}{c}\)   (division, \(c \neq 0\))
Worked Example 1 — Solving a Linear Equation

Solve:  \(x + 36 = 1 - 4(x - 5)\)

1
Distribute and combine on the right: \(x + 36 = 1 - 4x + 20 = 21 - 4x\)
2
Subtract 36 from each member: \(x = -15 - 4x\)
3
Add \(4x\) to each member: \(5x = -15\)
4
Divide each member by 5: \(x = -3\)

Check:  Left: \(-3 + 36 = 33\).  Right: \(1 - 4(-3 - 5) = 1 - 4(-8) = 1 + 32 = 33\).  \(\checkmark\)

Worked Example 2 — Solving for a Specified Variable

Solve for \(t\):  \(I = prt\)

1
Divide each member by \(pr\) (where \(p \neq 0\), \(r \neq 0\)):
$$t = \frac{I}{pr}$$
Worked Example 3 — Solving for \(g\)

Solve for \(g\):  \(d = \tfrac{1}{2}gt^{2}\)

1
Multiply both sides by 2: \(2d = gt^{2}\)
2
Divide both sides by \(t^{2}\): \(\displaystyle g = \frac{2d}{t^{2}}\)

5-2 The Properties of Inequality

For every pair of directed numbers \(a\) and \(b\), exactly one of the following is true:

$$a < b, \qquad a = b, \qquad a > b$$

This is the order property (trichotomy). On the number line, \(a < b\) means \(a\) lies to the left of \(b\).

If \(a\) lies to the left of \(b\), then \(a < b\).
Three Key Properties of Inequality

1. Transitive Property:
If \(a < b\) and \(b < c\), then \(a < c\).

2. Additive Property:
If \(a < b\), then \(a + c < b + c\) and \(a - c < b - c\) for any \(c\).

3. Multiplicative Property (the crucial one!):
If \(a < b\) and \(c > 0\), then \(ac < bc\).  (order preserved)
If \(a < b\) and \(c < 0\), then \(ac > bc\).  (order reversed!)

Caution — Multiplying or Dividing by a Negative

When you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, you must reverse the direction of the inequality sign. For example, from \(-4 < 3\), multiplying by \(-2\) gives \(8 > -6\).

Worked Example 4 — Solving an Inequality

Solve:  \(3 - 2x \le 11\)

1
Subtract 3 from each member: \(-2x \le 8\)
2
Divide by \(-2\) and reverse the sign: \(x \ge -4\)

The solution set is all directed numbers greater than or equal to \(-4\).

Solution set of \(x \ge -4\): closed dot at \(-4\), shading to the right.

5-3 Pairs of Inequalities

Sometimes a variable must satisfy two inequalities at the same time (an "and" condition), or at least one of two inequalities (an "or" condition).

Compound "And" Inequalities

The sentence \(-2 < x + 4 < 5\) means both \(-2 < x + 4\) and \(x + 4 < 5\) must hold simultaneously. Subtract 4 from every member:

$$-6 < x < 1$$
\(-6 < x < 1\): open dots at both endpoints, segment shaded between.

Compound "Or" Inequalities

The inequality \(|2y - 1| > 5\) is satisfied when either \(2y - 1 > 5\) or \(2y - 1 < -5\). Solving each:

$$y > 3 \quad\text{or}\quad y < -2$$
\(y < -2\) or \(y > 3\): two disjoint rays, open dots at the boundary points.
Key Insight — And vs. Or

"And" gives the intersection of two solution sets (values satisfying both). "Or" gives the union (values satisfying at least one). For example, the set satisfying both \(x < 3\) and \(x > 3\) is the empty set, while the set satisfying \(x < 3\) or \(x > 3\) is all directed numbers except 3.

5-4 A Plan for Solving Problems

Every word problem in this chapter—and beyond—follows the same four-step strategy:

The Four-Step Plan
  1. Read — What does the problem ask? Identify the unknown and let a variable represent it.
  2. Plan — What facts does the problem give? If helpful, draw a sketch or make a chart. Express other quantities in terms of your variable.
  3. Solve — Write an equation (or inequality) from the given facts and solve it.
  4. Check — Verify your answer in the original problem (not just the equation).
Worked Example 5 — Problem-Solving Plan

Problem: A roll has 10 fewer calories than twice the number in a slice of white bread. Together they contain at least 185 calories. Find the smallest possible number of calories in the bread.

1
Let \(x =\) calories in the bread. Then \(2x - 10 =\) calories in the roll.
2
Write the inequality: \((2x - 10) + x \ge 185\)
3
Simplify: \(3x - 10 \ge 185\), so \(3x \ge 195\), giving \(x \ge 65\).
4
Check: If \(x = 65\), roll = \(2(65) - 10 = 120\). Total = \(65 + 120 = 185 \ge 185\). \(\checkmark\)

The smallest possible number of calories in the bread is 65.

5-5 Problems about Consecutive Integers

Definitions

An integer is any whole number (positive, negative, or zero). An integer is even if it equals twice some integer; otherwise it is odd.

  • Consecutive integers: \(n,\; n+1,\; n+2,\; \ldots\)
  • Consecutive even integers: \(n,\; n+2,\; n+4,\; \ldots\)  (\(n\) even)
  • Consecutive odd integers: \(n,\; n+2,\; n+4,\; \ldots\)  (\(n\) odd)

Note: consecutive evens and consecutive odds each differ by 2.

Worked Example 6 — Consecutive Integers

Find three consecutive integers whose sum is 48.

1
Let \(n =\) the first integer. Then \(n+1\) and \(n+2\) are the next two.
2
Write the equation: \(n + (n+1) + (n+2) = 48\).
3
Simplify: \(3n + 3 = 48\), so \(3n = 45\), giving \(n = 15\).
4
The integers are \(15, 16, 17\).  Check: \(15 + 16 + 17 = 48\). \(\checkmark\)

5-6 Problems about Angles

Definitions

A directed angle consists of two rays from a common vertex, plus the rotation from one ray to the other. Counterclockwise rotation is positive; clockwise is negative. A degree (\(1°\)) is \(\frac{1}{360}\) of a complete rotation.

  • Complementary angles: two angles whose measures sum to \(90°\). If an angle measures \(n°\), its complement is \((90 - n)°\).
  • Supplementary angles: two angles whose measures sum to \(180°\). If an angle measures \(n°\), its supplement is \((180 - n)°\).
  • Triangle angle sum: the measures of the three angles of any triangle sum to \(180°\).
Worked Example 7 — Supplement and Complement

How large is an angle whose supplement is 21° less than four times its complement?

1
Let \(n =\) the number of degrees in the angle. Its complement is \(90 - n\); its supplement is \(180 - n\).
2
Translate: \(180 - n = 4(90 - n) - 21\).
3
Expand: \(180 - n = 360 - 4n - 21 = 339 - 4n\).
4
Add \(4n\): \(180 + 3n = 339\), so \(3n = 159\), giving \(n = 53\).

Check: Complement = \(37°\); supplement = \(127°\). Is \(127 = 4(37) - 21 = 148 - 21 = 127\)?  \(\checkmark\)

The angle is 53°.

5-7 Uniform Motion Problems

Fundamental Relationship
$$\text{distance} = \text{rate} \times \text{time} \qquad d = r \cdot t$$

Uniform-motion problems come in three main types:

  • Opposite directions: Two objects leave a point (or two points) traveling away from each other. Their combined distances equal the total separation.
  • Same direction: A faster object starts later and overtakes a slower one. Both cover the same distance at the moment they meet.
  • Round trip: The outbound distance equals the return distance.
Worked Example 8 — Opposite Directions

Problem: Mr. Rush's jet travels at 600 mph; Mr. Slow's plane travels at 320 mph. They leave airports 1380 miles apart at the same time, heading toward each other. In how many hours will they meet?

1
Let \(n\) = hours until they meet.
2

Their distances sum to 1380: \(600n + 320n = 1380\), so \(920n = 1380\), giving \(n = \frac{3}{2} = 1\tfrac{1}{2}\) hours.
4
Check: \(600 \times 1.5 = 900\); \(320 \times 1.5 = 480\). Total: \(900 + 480 = 1380\). \(\checkmark\)

They meet in \(1\frac{1}{2}\) hours.

Worked Example 9 — Same Direction (Overtaking)

Problem: An airplane at 350 mph passes an airport at 8 AM. A jet following the same course passes the airport at 10 AM and overtakes the airplane at noon. What is the jet's speed?

1
Let \(x =\) the jet's rate in mph.
2
Airplane flew 4 hours (8 AM to noon); jet flew 2 hours (10 AM to noon). At the overtaking point, both have covered the same distance.
3
Set distances equal: \(350 \times 4 = x \times 2\), so \(1400 = 2x\), giving \(x = 700\) mph.
4
Check: Airplane distance = \(350 \times 4 = 1400\) mi; jet distance = \(700 \times 2 = 1400\) mi. \(\checkmark\)

The jet was flying at 700 mph.

5-8 Mixture Problems

In mixture and value problems, the sum of the values (or quantities) of the individual ingredients must equal the value (or quantity) of the final mixture.

Mixture Principle
$$\underbrace{\text{amount}_1 \times \text{value}_1}_{\text{Ingredient 1}} + \underbrace{\text{amount}_2 \times \text{value}_2}_{\text{Ingredient 2}} = \underbrace{\text{total amount} \times \text{blend value}}_{\text{Mixture}}$$
Worked Example 10 — Book Fair Mixture

Problem: At a Book Fair, 600 books were sold: pocket editions at 35¢ each and hard-covers at 50¢ each. Total receipts matched last year's when 600 books sold at an average of 40¢ each. How many of each kind were sold?

1
Let \(n =\) number of pocket editions. Then \(600 - n =\) hard-covers.

400 pocket editions and 200 hard-covered books were sold.

Practice Problems

Practice 1 — Transforming Equations

Solve for \(x\):  \(5(x + 1) = 4(x + 2)\)

Show Solution

\(5x + 5 = 4x + 8\)

Subtract \(4x\):  \(x + 5 = 8\)

Subtract 5:  \(x = 3\)

Check: LHS \(= 5(4) = 20\). RHS \(= 4(5) = 20\). \(\checkmark\)

Practice 2 — Inequality

Solve and graph:  \(8x + 2 > 5x - 4\)

Show Solution

Subtract \(5x\):  \(3x + 2 > -4\)

Subtract 2:  \(3x > -6\)

Divide by 3:  \(x > -2\)

Graph: open dot at \(-2\), shade to the right.

Practice 3 — Consecutive Odd Integers

Find three consecutive odd integers such that the sum of the first and third exceeds the second by 27.

Show Solution

Let the integers be \(n\), \(n+2\), \(n+4\).

\(n + (n + 4) = (n + 2) + 27\)

\(2n + 4 = n + 29\)

\(n = 25\)

The integers are 25, 27, 29.

Check: \(25 + 29 = 54 = 27 + 27\). \(\checkmark\)

Practice 4 — Angles

An angle is 12° more than its complement. Find the complement.

Show Solution

Let \(n =\) the angle. Then \(90 - n =\) the complement.

\(n = (90 - n) + 12\)

\(n = 102 - n\), so \(2n = 102\), giving \(n = 51\).

Complement \(= 90 - 51 = \mathbf{39°}\).

Practice 5 — Uniform Motion

A train leaves Omaha at 9 AM traveling at 50 mph. At 1 PM a plane leaves Omaha in the same direction at 300 mph. At what time does the plane overtake the train?

Show Solution

When the plane catches the train, both have traveled the same distance from Omaha.

Let \(t =\) hours the plane has flown. The train has been traveling for \(t + 4\) hours.

\(300t = 50(t + 4)\)

\(300t = 50t + 200\)

\(250t = 200\), so \(t = 0.8\) hours = 48 minutes.

The plane overtakes the train at 1:48 PM.

Practice 6 — Mixture

A confectioner makes 100 pounds of candy to sell at \$1.75/lb by mixing candy worth \$1.65/lb with some worth \$1.90/lb. How many pounds of each?

Show Solution

Let \(x =\) pounds of \$1.65 candy. Then \(100 - x =\) pounds of \$1.90 candy.

\(1.65x + 1.90(100 - x) = 1.75(100)\)

\(1.65x + 190 - 1.90x = 175\)

\(-0.25x = -15\), so \(x = 60\).

60 pounds of \$1.65 candy and 40 pounds of \$1.90 candy.

Quick Reference

Solving Equations

Add, subtract, multiply, or divide both sides by the same nonzero number. The solution set is unchanged.

Inequality Rules

Add/subtract freely. Multiply/divide by a positive number: sign stays. Multiply/divide by a negative: reverse the sign.

Compound Inequalities

And = intersection (both true).
Or = union (at least one true).
\(|x| < a \iff -a < x < a\)
\(|x| > a \iff x < -a \;\text{or}\; x > a\)

Consecutive Integers

General: \(n, n{+}1, n{+}2, \ldots\)
Even/Odd: \(n, n{+}2, n{+}4, \ldots\)

Angles

Complement: \(90 - n\)
Supplement: \(180 - n\)
Triangle sum: \(180°\)

Uniform Motion

\(d = r \cdot t\)
Opposite dirs: \(d_1 + d_2 = \text{total}\)
Same dir: \(d_1 = d_2\) at meeting

Mixtures

\(\text{amt}_1 \cdot \text{val}_1 + \text{amt}_2 \cdot \text{val}_2 = \text{total amt} \cdot \text{blend val}\)

Problem-Solving Plan

1. Read (identify unknown)
2. Plan (sketch, chart, expressions)
3. Solve (equation or inequality)
4. Check (in original problem)
