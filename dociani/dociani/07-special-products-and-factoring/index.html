7-1

Factoring Integers

Factoring is the process of writing a number (or expression) as a product of simpler pieces. We begin with integers before moving to polynomials.

Definition — Prime Number
A prime number is an integer greater than 1 whose only positive integral factors are itself and 1. The first several primes are: $$2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 11,\ 13,\ 17,\ 19,\ 23,\ \ldots$$
Definition — Prime Factorization
Every integer greater than 1 can be written as a product of prime numbers in exactly one way (up to the order of the factors). This is the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.

Example: To factor 392 into primes, systematically try the smallest prime factor at each stage:

$$392 = 2 \times 196 = 2 \times 2 \times 98 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 49 = 2^3 \times 7^2$$
Definition — Greatest Common Factor (GCF)
The greatest common factor of two or more integers is the largest integer that divides each of them. To find the GCF, take each common prime factor raised to the smallest power that appears in any of the factorizations.

Example: Find the GCF of 392 and 1260.

$$392 = 2^3 \cdot 7^2 \qquad\qquad 1260 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 \cdot 7$$

Common primes: 2 and 7. Smallest powers: \(2^2\) and \(7^1\).

$$\text{GCF} = 2^2 \cdot 7 = 28$$
7-2

Dividing Monomials

To divide one monomial by another, divide the numerical coefficients and apply the quotient-of-powers rule for each variable:

Rule — Quotient of Powers
For any nonzero base \(b\) and positive integers \(m > n\): $$\frac{b^m}{b^n} = b^{m-n}$$

Examples:

$$\frac{12x^5y^3}{4x^2y} = 3x^{5-2}y^{3-1} = 3x^3y^2$$ $$\frac{-18a^4b^2}{6a^3b^2} = -3a$$
7-3

Monomial Factors of Polynomials

The first step in factoring any polynomial is to look for a greatest common monomial factor—a monomial that divides every term.

Strategy — GCF Factoring
Use the distributive property in reverse: $$ab + ac = a(b + c)$$ Always extract the greatest common monomial factor (largest coefficient and highest degree in each variable).

Examples:

$$4ab + 6a = 2a(2b + 3)$$ $$12x^4 - 8x^2 + 20x = 4x(3x^3 - 2x + 5)$$ $$18a^3c^2 - 9a^2c + 6ac^3 = 3ac(6a^2c - 3a + 2c^2)$$
Key Insight — Factoring by Grouping
When a polynomial has four terms and no single common factor, try grouping pairs of terms and factoring each pair separately: $$3x^2 - 2x + 6x - 4 = x(3x - 2) + 2(3x - 2) = (x + 2)(3x - 2)$$
7-4

Multiplying the Sum and Difference of Two Numbers

When you multiply the sum and the difference of the same two quantities, the middle terms cancel, leaving the difference of two squares.

Formula — Difference of Squares Product
$$(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2$$
Figure 7-1: The area \(a^2 - b^2\) can be rearranged into a rectangle of dimensions \((a+b)\) by \((a-b)\).

Examples:

$$(x + 3)(x - 3) = x^2 - 9$$ $$(5a + 2b)(5a - 2b) = 25a^2 - 4b^2$$ $$(3m + 7t^2)(3m - 7t^2) = 9m^2 - 49t^4$$
7-5

Factoring the Difference of Two Squares

Reading the formula from right to left gives us a factoring rule. Whenever you see the difference of two perfect squares, factor immediately.

Formula — Factoring Difference of Squares
$$a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)$$
Key Insight — Recognizing Squares
A monomial like \(49x^2y^4\) is a perfect square when its numerical coefficient is a perfect square integer and every variable has an even exponent: \(49x^2y^4 = (7xy^2)^2\).

Examples:

$$m^2 - 16 = (m + 4)(m - 4)$$ $$36x^2 - y^2 = (6x + y)(6x - y)$$
Caution
The sum of two squares, \(a^2 + b^2\), cannot be factored over the integers. Only the difference factors. Also, always check for a common factor first: $$2a - 8a^3 = 2a(1 - 4a^2) = 2a(1 + 2a)(1 - 2a)$$

Squaring a Binomial

The square of a binomial appears so frequently that you should learn to write it at sight. Study the geometric model and the algebraic pattern together.

Figure 7-2: The area model shows that the square with side \((a + b)\) equals \(a^2 + 2ab + b^2\).
Formulas — Squaring a Binomial
Square of a sum: $$(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$$ Square of a difference: $$(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$$
Procedure:
  1. Square the first term.
  2. Double the product of the two terms.
  3. Square the second term.

Examples:

$$(x + 1)^2 = x^2 + 2x + 1$$ $$(y - 1)^2 = y^2 - 2y + 1$$ $$(2n + 3)^2 = 4n^2 + 12n + 9$$ $$(4m^2 - n^3)^2 = 16m^4 - 8m^2n^3 + n^6$$

Factoring a Perfect Square Trinomial

Before using the squaring formulas in reverse to factor, you must verify that the trinomial really is a perfect square.

Test — Is It a Perfect Square Trinomial?
A trinomial \(A + B + C\) is a perfect square if:
  1. The first term \(A\) is a perfect square.
  2. The third term \(C\) is a perfect square.
  3. The middle term \(B\) equals twice the product of the square roots of \(A\) and \(C\).

Example: Is \(x^2 + 14x + 49\) a perfect square trinomial?

  • \(x^2 = (x)^2\) — yes, a perfect square.
  • \(49 = 7^2\) — yes, a perfect square.
  • \(14x = 2(x)(7)\) — yes, double the product.

Therefore:

$$x^2 + 14x + 49 = (x + 7)^2$$

More examples:

$$196m^2 - 476mn + 289n^2 = (14m - 17n)^2$$ $$y^6 - 8y^3 + 16 = (y^3 - 4)^2$$
7-8

Factoring Trinomials: Type \(x^2 + bx + c\)

To factor a trinomial whose leading coefficient is 1, find two integers \(r\) and \(s\) whose product equals the constant term and whose sum equals the coefficient of the middle term.

Method — Factoring \(x^2 + bx + c\)
Find integers \(r\) and \(s\) such that: $$r \cdot s = c \qquad\text{and}\qquad r + s = b$$ Then: $$x^2 + bx + c = (x + r)(x + s)$$
Key Insight — Sign Rules
  • If \(c > 0\) and \(b > 0\): both \(r\) and \(s\) are positive.
  • If \(c > 0\) and \(b < 0\): both \(r\) and \(s\) are negative.
  • If \(c < 0\): one of \(r, s\) is positive and the other is negative; the sign of \(b\) tells you which has greater absolute value.

Example: Factor \(x^2 + 5x + 6\).

We need \(r \cdot s = 6\) and \(r + s = 5\). Both positive. The pairs: \(1 \times 6\) (sum 7) and \(2 \times 3\) (sum 5). The second works.

$$x^2 + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)$$

Example: Factor \(x^2 - 11x + 24\).

Need \(r \cdot s = 24\) and \(r + s = -11\). Both negative. Pairs: \((-3)(-8)\) gives sum \(-11\). That works.

$$x^2 - 11x + 24 = (x - 3)(x - 8)$$
Definition — Prime Polynomial
A polynomial that cannot be factored into polynomials of lower degree with integral coefficients is called prime (or irreducible) over that set. For example, \(x^2 + 10x + 8\) is prime over the integers because no integer pair with product 8 has sum 10.
7-9

Factoring Trinomials: Type \(ax^2 + bx + c\)

When the leading coefficient \(a \neq 1\), factoring requires inspection and trial. You must find binomials whose first terms multiply to \(ax^2\) and whose last terms multiply to \(c\), while the cross products sum to \(bx\).

Strategy — Trial and Check
To factor \(ax^2 + bx + c\):
  1. List factor pairs of \(a\) (for the first terms of the binomials).
  2. List factor pairs of \(c\) (for the last terms).
  3. Use the sign rules to determine whether binomial factors are sums, differences, or a mix.
  4. Test combinations until the cross-product sum equals \(bx\).

Example: Factor \(6x^2 - 25x + 14\).

Since the constant is positive and the linear term is negative, both binomial factors are differences. Testing \((2x - 7)(3x - 2)\):

$$\underbrace{(2x)(3x)}_{6x^2}\quad \underbrace{(2x)(-2) + (-7)(3x)}_{-4x - 21x = -25x}\quad \underbrace{(-7)(-2)}_{14}$$ $$6x^2 - 25x + 14 = (2x - 7)(3x - 2)$$
Key Insight — Eliminating Possibilities
If the original trinomial has no common factor, then none of its binomial factors can have a common factor either. This lets you skip many trial combinations. For instance, \(6x - 14 = 2(3x - 7)\) has a common factor 2, so it cannot be a factor of a trinomial with no common factor.

Combining Types of Factoring

Real problems often require multiple factoring techniques applied in sequence. Always follow this master strategy:

Master Factoring Strategy
  1. Common factor first: Extract the GCF of all terms.
  2. Count the terms:
    • 2 terms — check for difference of squares.
    • 3 terms — check for perfect square trinomial, then general trinomial factoring.
    • 4 terms — try factoring by grouping.
  3. Factor completely: Examine each factor to see if it can be factored further.
  4. Check by multiplying the factors back together.

Examples:

$$3x^2 - 27 = 3(x^2 - 9) = 3(x + 3)(x - 3)$$ $$2w^2 + 36w + 162 = 2(w^2 + 18w + 81) = 2(w + 9)^2$$ $$2a^4 - 32 = 2(a^4 - 16) = 2(a^2 + 4)(a^2 - 4) = 2(a^2 + 4)(a + 2)(a - 2)$$
Caution — Factor Completely!
Do not stop too early. After each step, inspect every factor to see if it can be factored further. In the example above, \(a^2 - 4\) is itself a difference of squares and must be factored one more time. However, \(a^2 + 4\) is a sum of squares and is prime.

Solving Polynomial Equations by Factoring

Zero-Product Property
A product is zero if, and only if, at least one of the factors is zero. $$ab = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a = 0 \ \text{ or }\ b = 0$$
Procedure — Solving by Factoring
  1. Write the equation in standard form (one side equal to zero).
  2. Factor the nonzero side completely.
  3. Set each factor equal to zero.
  4. Solve the resulting linear equations.
  5. Check each root in the original equation.
Key Insight — Terminology
  • A quadratic equation has degree 2 (e.g., \(x^2 - 20x - 300 = 0\)).
  • A linear equation has degree 1.
  • A cubic equation has degree 3.
If a variable factor appears (e.g., \(x\)), do not divide it out—it may give a root of zero!

Worked Problems

Problem 1 — Prime Factorization and GCF
Find the greatest common factor of 180 and 252.
Solution
Factor each into primes: $$180 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 \qquad 252 = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 \cdot 7$$
Common prime factors with smallest exponents: \(2^2\) and \(3^2\).
$$\text{GCF} = 2^2 \cdot 3^2 = 4 \cdot 9 = \boxed{36}$$
Problem 2 — Factoring a Difference of Two Squares
Factor completely: \(2a - 8a^3\)
Solution
Extract the GCF first: $$2a - 8a^3 = 2a(1 - 4a^2)$$
Recognize \(1 - 4a^2\) as a difference of squares: \(1^2 - (2a)^2\).
$$2a(1 - 4a^2) = \boxed{2a(1 + 2a)(1 - 2a)}$$
Problem 3 — Factoring a Perfect Square Trinomial
Factor: \(196m^2 - 476mn + 289n^2\)
Solution
Check: Is \(196m^2\) a perfect square? Yes: \((14m)^2\).
Is \(289n^2\) a perfect square? Yes: \((17n)^2\).
Is the middle term \(2 \times 14m \times 17n = 476mn\)? Yes.
Since the middle term is negative, this is the square of a difference: $$196m^2 - 476mn + 289n^2 = \boxed{(14m - 17n)^2}$$
Problem 4 — Factoring a General Trinomial (\(ax^2 + bx + c\))
Factor: \(8x^2 + 2x - 3\)
Solution
Since \(c = -3 < 0\), the binomial factors are one sum and one difference.
Factor pairs of \(8\): \(\{1,8\}\), \(\{2,4\}\). Factor pairs of \(3\): \(\{1,3\}\).
Try \((2x - 1)(4x + 3)\): $$\text{Cross terms: } (2x)(3) + (-1)(4x) = 6x - 4x = 2x \quad\checkmark$$
$$8x^2 + 2x - 3 = \boxed{(2x - 1)(4x + 3)}$$
Problem 5 — Solving a Quadratic Equation by Factoring
Solve: \(x^2 - 20x = 300\)
Solution
Step 1. Standard form: $$x^2 - 20x - 300 = 0$$
Step 2. Factor: we need two numbers with product \(-300\) and sum \(-20\). Try \(-30\) and \(+10\): \((-30)(10) = -300\) and \(-30 + 10 = -20\). Yes! $$(x - 30)(x + 10) = 0$$
Step 3. Set each factor to zero: $$x - 30 = 0 \implies x = 30 \qquad x + 10 = 0 \implies x = -10$$
Step 4. Check: \(30^2 - 20(30) = 900 - 600 = 300\) ✓
\((-10)^2 - 20(-10) = 100 + 200 = 300\) ✓
$$\text{Solution set} = \boxed{\{30,\ -10\}}$$
Problem 6 — Multi-Step Factoring and Solving
Solve: \(x^3 = 4x^2 + 5x\)
Solution
Step 1. Standard form: $$x^3 - 4x^2 - 5x = 0$$
Step 2. Factor out the common factor \(x\): $$x(x^2 - 4x - 5) = 0$$
Step 3. Factor the trinomial: need product \(-5\), sum \(-4\). Use \(-5\) and \(+1\): $$x(x - 5)(x + 1) = 0$$
Step 4. Set each factor to zero: $$x = 0, \qquad x - 5 = 0 \implies x = 5, \qquad x + 1 = 0 \implies x = -1$$
Step 5. Check all three in \(x^3 = 4x^2 + 5x\):
  • \(0^3 = 0 = 4(0) + 5(0)\) ✓
  • \(5^3 = 125 = 4(25) + 5(5) = 100 + 25\) ✓
  • \((-1)^3 = -1 = 4(1) + 5(-1) = 4 - 5\) ✓
$$\text{Solution set} = \boxed{\{0,\ 5,\ -1\}}$$
